CLARITY Act explained: what the crypto market bill would do and why it stalled

The CLARITY Act, H.R. 3633, would write into law which crypto assets and platforms the SEC oversees and which fall to the CFTC. As of November 2, 2026, it is stuck in the Senate after a 49 to 50 vote to start debate failed on September 15.

Perpetual futures funding rates explained: how 0.05% can cost 55% a year

A funding rate is a small payment that traders of perpetual futures make to each other to keep the contract's price close to the real market price. When it is positive, longs pay shorts. When it is negative, shorts pay longs.

How crypto airdrops work, and how to spot the scams that copy them

An airdrop is a free drop of tokens to wallets, usually as a reward for early users. Real airdrops never ask for your seed phrase. Most scams instead trick you into signing a transaction on a fake claim site.

What is a bitcoin ETF? How spot funds hold coins and what daily flows mean

A spot bitcoin ETF is a fund that holds real bitcoin and trades on a stock exchange like a share. Big brokers create and redeem its shares in blocks, and the daily flow numbers measure exactly those creations and redemptions.

What is Base? How Coinbase's $14.7B layer 2 works and who can change it

Base is an Ethereum layer 2, an optimistic rollup built with the OP Stack, and the biggest by value: about $14.7 billion secured as of November 2, 2026, per L2BEAT. It runs transactions cheaply, posts the data to Ethereum, and a Coinbase multisig holds one of its two upgrade keys.

What is proof of reserves? What it proves about exchanges, and what it misses

Proof of reserves is a public check that an exchange holds at least as much crypto as its customers have deposited. Merkle trees or zero-knowledge proofs let you confirm your balance was counted, but it is a one-day snapshot, not proof the company is solvent.

What is x402? The HTTP payment protocol that lets AI agents pay per request

x402 is an open protocol that uses the web's "402 Payment Required" status code so apps and AI agents can pay for an API call or a web page in stablecoins, inside the request itself, with no account or card form.

MiCA explained: the EU crypto law, CASP licenses, stablecoin rules and key dates

MiCA is the EU's rulebook for crypto: firms that offer crypto services need a license from a national regulator, and stablecoins face strict reserve and supervision rules. It has applied in full since December 30, 2024, and the last transition window closed on July 1, 2026.
