South Korea crypto rules explained: cold wallets, bank-held cash and jail terms

Crypto exchanges in South Korea must register with the Financial Intelligence Unit and, since July 19, 2024, follow the Virtual Asset User Protection Act: customer cash held at banks, most coins in cold wallets, and prison terms for market manipulation.

FDV vs market cap: how to read a token's supply, with HYPE's 4x gap as a guide

Market cap is price times the tokens circulating today. Fully diluted valuation (FDV) is price times the whole supply, including locked tokens. The gap between them shows how much supply is still to come.

How to check and revoke token approvals before an old one drains your wallet

Look up your wallet address in an approval checker such as Etherscan's Token Approvals page or Revoke.cash, find spenders you no longer use or do not recognize, and revoke them. Each revoke is an on-chain transaction, so it costs a small network fee.

CEX vs DEX explained: who holds your crypto, and what can go wrong

A centralized exchange (CEX) is a company that holds your coins and matches orders in an order book; a decentralized exchange (DEX) is blockchain software that lets you swap from your own wallet against a pool of tokens. The real trade-off is custody: convenience and support on one side, control and responsibility on the other.

What is a layer 2? Rollups, proofs and why no big L2 is Stage 2 yet

A layer 2 is a separate blockchain that runs transactions cheaply and settles them on Ethereum, using fraud proofs or validity proofs to show its state is correct. L2BEAT tracks about $30 billion in L2s, and its Stages show how much control each team still holds.

Prediction markets explained: how Kalshi and Polymarket work, and the real risks

A prediction market lets people trade contracts that pay $1 if an event happens and $0 if it does not, so prices read like odds. Kalshi is a CFTC-regulated US exchange; most Polymarket markets settle on chain through the UMA oracle.

Singapore crypto rules explained: who needs a MAS license, and who won't get one

Crypto firms serving customers in Singapore need a license from the Monetary Authority of Singapore, mainly under the Payment Services Act 2019. Since June 30, 2025, Singapore firms that serve only overseas customers also need a license, and MAS says it will generally not grant one.

Crypto options expiry and max pain explained: what happens at 3 a.m. ET

Crypto options on Deribit, a major options exchange, expire at 3 a.m. ET (4 a.m. ET during daylight saving time) and settle in cash against a 30-minute average of an index price. Max pain is the strike price at which the most option value would expire worthless, a number traders watch but one that does not control the price.

Token unlocks explained: cliffs, vesting and why unlocked does not mean sold

A token unlock is when tokens locked at launch, usually for the team, investors or a treasury, become free to move. Unlocks add to circulating supply and dilute holders, but an unlocked token is not a sold one: Hyperliquid insiders claimed far less HYPE than they could.

What is a wallet drainer? How one signature can empty your crypto wallet

A wallet drainer is a scam kit that tricks you into signing an approval or permit that lets the attacker move your tokens. Your seed phrase never leaves your hands: your own signature does the damage, sometimes days later.

Bitcoin dominance and crypto market cap explained: why sites say 58% or 60%

Total crypto market cap adds up the market value of every tracked coin, and bitcoin dominance is bitcoin's share of it. As of November 2, 2026, the total was about $2.6 trillion to $2.7 trillion and bitcoin's share about 58% to 60%, depending on the data site.

Hong Kong crypto licensing explained: SFC exchange rules, 2 of 36 stablecoin bids

In Hong Kong, crypto exchanges need a license from the Securities and Futures Commission, and anyone issuing a fiat-backed stablecoin needs one from the Hong Kong Monetary Authority. Only two stablecoin issuers had been licensed as of April 2026: HSBC and Anchorpoint Financial.
