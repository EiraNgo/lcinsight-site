How stolen crypto is traced and frozen, and why thieves dump stablecoins fast

Every blockchain transfer is public, so investigators can follow stolen funds hop by hop. Money can be stopped in two places: stablecoin issuers like Tether and Circle can freeze tokens on-chain, and exchanges can lock accounts when stolen funds arrive.

Seed phrase and hardware wallet safety: the words that guard your crypto

Your seed phrase is the master key to your wallet: anyone who sees it can take everything, and if you lose it, nobody can restore it. A hardware wallet keeps keys offline, but only if the phrase stays offline too and the device is genuine.

Japan crypto rules explained: FSA registration, 95% cold storage and 2x leverage

Crypto exchanges in Japan must register with the Financial Services Agency under the Payment Services Act, keep at least 95% of customer coins offline, and cap retail leverage at 2x. A 2026 government bill would revise the rules again, borrowing from securities law.

November 2, 2026

South Korea crypto rules explained: cold wallets, bank-held cash and jail terms

Crypto exchanges in South Korea must register with the Financial Intelligence Unit and, since July 19, 2024, follow the Virtual Asset User Protection Act: customer cash held at banks, most coins in cold wallets, and prison terms for market manipulation.

How to check and revoke token approvals before an old one drains your wallet

Look up your wallet address in an approval checker such as Etherscan's Token Approvals page or Revoke.cash, find spenders you no longer use or do not recognize, and revoke them. Each revoke is an on-chain transaction, so it costs a small network fee.

Prediction markets explained: how Kalshi and Polymarket work, and the real risks

A prediction market lets people trade contracts that pay $1 if an event happens and $0 if it does not, so prices read like odds. Kalshi is a CFTC-regulated US exchange; most Polymarket markets settle on chain through the UMA oracle.

November 2, 2026

Singapore crypto rules explained: who needs a MAS license, and who won't get one

Crypto firms serving customers in Singapore need a license from the Monetary Authority of Singapore, mainly under the Payment Services Act 2019. Since June 30, 2025, Singapore firms that serve only overseas customers also need a license, and MAS says it will generally not grant one.

SEC vs. CFTC: who regulates crypto in the US, and where the line now falls

The SEC oversees crypto that counts as a security, and the CFTC oversees commodities like bitcoin and ether plus derivatives on them. Since March 2026, the two agencies share an interpretation that says most crypto assets are not securities.

How crypto exchange hacks happen: the weak spot is rarely the key itself

Most exchange hacks start in hot wallets, the online wallets that pay out withdrawals. Attackers rarely break the cryptography. They get into the systems and people that approve transactions, then rush to swap stablecoins before issuers can freeze them.

November 2, 2026

How crypto liquidations work: why a 10% move can wipe out a 10x trade

A liquidation is when an exchange force-closes a leveraged trade because losses have eaten through the trader's collateral. It protects the exchange and other traders from debts the trader cannot pay.

November 2, 2026

Perpetual futures funding rates explained: how 0.05% can cost 55% a year

A funding rate is a small payment that traders of perpetual futures make to each other to keep the contract's price close to the real market price. When it is positive, longs pay shorts. When it is negative, shorts pay longs.

November 2, 2026

How crypto airdrops work, and how to spot the scams that copy them

An airdrop is a free drop of tokens to wallets, usually as a reward for early users. Real airdrops never ask for your seed phrase. Most scams instead trick you into signing a transaction on a fake claim site.

November 2, 2026

MiCA explained: the EU crypto law, CASP licenses, stablecoin rules and key dates

MiCA is the EU's rulebook for crypto: firms that offer crypto services need a license from a national regulator, and stablecoins face strict reserve and supervision rules. It has applied in full since December 30, 2024, and the last transition window closed on July 1, 2026.
