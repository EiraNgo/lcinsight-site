What is Bitcoin? How it works, why only 21 million exist and the risks

Bitcoin is a digital currency that moves directly between users on a public network, with no bank or company in the middle. Miners secure it with computing power, its supply is capped at 21 million coins, and BTC traded near $77,323 as of November 2, 2026.

Bitcoin mining and hashrate explained: difficulty, hashprice and data sources

Bitcoin miners race to find a block hash below a target, and every 2,016 blocks (about two weeks) the network resets the difficulty so blocks keep coming about every 10 minutes. Hashrate, the total guessing power, cannot be measured directly; it is estimated from difficulty and block times.

Crypto options expiry and max pain explained: what happens at 3 a.m. ET

Crypto options on Deribit, a major options exchange, expire at 3 a.m. ET (4 a.m. ET during daylight saving time) and settle in cash against a 30-minute average of an index price. Max pain is the strike price at which the most option value would expire worthless, a number traders watch but one that does not control the price.

Bitcoin halving explained: what it is, when the next one is and why it matters

The bitcoin halving is a rule in bitcoin's code that cuts the reward for mining a new block in half every 210,000 blocks, about every four years. The last one happened on April 20, 2024, at block 840,000. The next is due at block 1,050,000, expected around spring 2028.

How crypto liquidations work: why a 10% move can wipe out a 10x trade

A funding rate is a small payment that traders of perpetual futures make to each other to keep the contract's price close to the real market price. When it is positive, longs pay shorts. When it is negative, shorts pay longs.
