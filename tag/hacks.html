What is address poisoning? The copy-paste scam hiding in your wallet history

Address poisoning is a scam where an attacker plants a lookalike address in your transaction history and waits for you to copy it. No hacking is needed, only a tiny or fake transfer and a hurried user, and the payment cannot be reversed.

How to check and revoke token approvals before an old one drains your wallet

Look up your wallet address in an approval checker such as Etherscan's Token Approvals page or Revoke.cash, find spenders you no longer use or do not recognize, and revoke them. Each revoke is an on-chain transaction, so it costs a small network fee.

What is a wallet drainer? How one signature can empty your crypto wallet

A wallet drainer is a scam kit that tricks you into signing an approval or permit that lets the attacker move your tokens. Your seed phrase never leaves your hands: your own signature does the damage, sometimes days later.

How crypto exchange hacks happen: the weak spot is rarely the key itself

Most exchange hacks start in hot wallets, the online wallets that pay out withdrawals. Attackers rarely break the cryptography. They get into the systems and people that approve transactions, then rush to swap stablecoins before issuers can freeze them.

How crypto airdrops work, and how to spot the scams that copy them

An airdrop is a free drop of tokens to wallets, usually as a reward for early users. Real airdrops never ask for your seed phrase. Most scams instead trick you into signing a transaction on a fake claim site.
