Gonka's Ethereum bridge lost $884,000 in USDT in one minute, then 22 million WGNK were minted

A wallet funded six hours earlier set a new key on Gonka's Ethereum bridge, pulled out 884,150 USDT and minted 22 million wrapped GNK on November 18, 2026. The WGNK price fell about 84% by evening.

LC Insight Editorial Team3 min read

Key points

  • At 1:00 p.m. ET on November 18, 2026, a new wallet called a function Etherscan names Submit Group Key on Gonka's Ethereum bridge contract, which is also the WGNK token.
  • Within 72 seconds the same wallet withdrew 884,150.87 USDT and 647.74 USDC from the bridge and minted 22,000,000 WGNK out of nothing.
  • Uniswap trades then returned about 606.65 ETH to the wallet, and about 606.7 ETH left it for a second address by 3:19 p.m. ET. WGNK fell from $0.1428 to $0.0228 on CoinGecko.

What the chain shows

Gonka is an AI project with its own blockchain. Its coin, GNK, crosses to Ethereum through a bridge, and on Ethereum it trades as Wrapped Gonka (WGNK). Gonka's bridge documentation says one contract, 0x972a...2f68, is both the bridge and the WGNK token.

On Wednesday, November 18, 2026, a wallet, 0x6650...4D80, received 0.05 ETH at 7:08 a.m. ET. It had no other history. At 1:00 p.m. ET it sent its first real transaction to the bridge: a call Etherscan decodes as Submit Group Key. A group key is the shared signing key a bridge trusts to approve transfers.

What followed took 72 seconds. At 1:01 p.m. ET the wallet called withdraw and the bridge sent it 884,150.87 USDT, worth about $883,210. Twenty-four seconds later a second withdraw sent it 647.74 USDC. At 1:02 p.m. ET a call to mintWithSignature created 22,000,000 WGNK from the null address straight into the wallet, about $675,924 at the price Etherscan showed.

Where the money went

Minutes later the wallet approved USDT and USDC for trading and used the Uniswap router. Etherscan's internal transactions show about 606.65 ETH arriving from Uniswap contracts. The wallet then sent ETH out in three transfers: 10 ETH at 1:08 p.m. ET, 100 ETH at 1:14 p.m. ET and 496.70 ETH at 3:19 p.m. ET, all to one address beginning 0xdE356F65. By evening the first wallet held almost nothing.

At ETH's price on Etherscan that evening, about $1,793, the 606.7 ETH is worth roughly $1.09 million. DefiLlama's hacks list, as read by our watcher, files the incident at about $1.05 million with the technique "Bridge Logic Flaw". The bridge contract itself now holds about $37 in WGNK and $6 in ETH. Nobody has publicly named who controls either wallet, and we do not guess.

What WGNK did

WGNK traded near $0.143 all day before the mint. CoinGecko shows $0.1428 at 1 p.m. ET, $0.0625 an hour later and $0.0228 at 7:09 p.m. ET, a drop of about 84%. Anyone holding WGNK on Ethereum now holds a token whose supply grew by 22 million in one transaction.

LC Insight analysis

This section is our reading of the facts above, not a statement of fact.

  • We think the key change is the heart of this story. If one outside wallet could replace the key the bridge trusts, every later check would pass, and the USDT and the mint would look normal to the contract.
  • Wrapped tokens are only as good as the bridge behind them. A 22 million WGNK mint with nothing locked on the Gonka side breaks the one-for-one link holders rely on.
  • Speed matters. The money was out in 72 seconds and in ETH within minutes, which leaves almost no time for a stablecoin issuer to freeze it.

What's next

  • A statement or post-mortem from Gonka (we found none on its documentation site at publication)
  • Whether Tether or anyone freezes funds, and where the ETH at 0xdE356F65 moves next
  • How Gonka treats WGNK holders and whether the bridge reopens with a new key
