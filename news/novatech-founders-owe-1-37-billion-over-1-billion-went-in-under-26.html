NovaTech founders owe $1.37 billion. Over $1 billion went in, under $26 million was traded

New York's attorney general says a court ordered Cynthia and Eddy Petion to pay $1,367,023,585 over a crypto scheme that reached more than 11,000 New Yorkers and targeted Haitian communities. The couple left the country before the case was decided.

LC Insight Editorial Team3 min read

Key points

  • A New York court ordered NovaTech founders Cynthia and Eddy Petion to pay $1,367,023,585, Attorney General Letitia James said on November 25, 2026.
  • The proposed order the AG filed on November 24 breaks that into $1.13 billion in disgorgement (giving back money taken) plus $232.5 million in interest, owed jointly by the couple and three NovaTech companies.
  • More than $1 billion in crypto was deposited with NovaTech, but less than $26 million was ever traded on its platform, the AG said when it sued in June 2024.

What the court ordered

A New York court ordered the founders of crypto trading firm NovaTechFx (NovaTech), Cynthia and Eddy Petion, to pay a total of $1,367,023,585, Attorney General Letitia James announced on November 25, 2026. The AG calls NovaTech a crypto Ponzi scheme: one that paid early investors with money from later ones.

The case is in the Commercial Division of New York County Supreme Court (index no. 451633/2024). A default judgment came first. It was entered on August 4, 2025, after the couple left the country, according to the AG and the court papers, and it banned both of them for life from the securities, commodities and crypto industries. James said the scheme preyed on immigrant families by working through churches and community groups.

The numbers in the order

The copy of the order the AG posted is the proposed order it filed with the court, stamped received on November 24, 2026, with the judge's signature line still blank. The AG's release says the court has now ordered the total. The proposed order splits the money into three parts:

  • $1,367,023,585 owed jointly by Nova Tech Ltd, NovaTech Advisors LLC, NovaPay LLC, Cynthia Petion and Eddy Petion: $1,134,549,691 in disgorgement plus $232,473,894 in interest at 9% a year.
  • $4,694,712 owed by Cynthia Petion for promoting a second scheme, AWS Mining: $3 million plus interest.
  • $1,455,666 owed by AWS Mining Pty Ltd: $930,195 plus interest.

The proposed order does not say how many investors there were or how any money will reach them. The AG's release does not describe a claims process either.

How the scheme worked

When it sued in June 2024, the AG said NovaTech took deposits from August 2019 to April 2023 and promised weekly trading profits of about 2% to 4%, plus bonuses for bringing in new investors. More than $1 billion in crypto was deposited, but less than $26 million was traded on its platform. NovaTech collapsed in May 2023, and tens of thousands of investors could not withdraw.

AWS Mining, which collapsed in 2019, had promised 15% to 20% a month, according to the same lawsuit. The AG says the schemes were sold through prayer groups, social media and WhatsApp chats, reached hundreds of thousands of investors worldwide, including more than 11,000 New Yorkers, and went after Haitian communities in particular.

Others in the case

The AG earlier secured nearly $6 million from four other people involved in the scheme, who are also banned for life from the securities, commodities and crypto industries.

LC Insight analysis

This section is our reading of the facts above, not a statement of fact.

  • We think the key number is the gap: over $1 billion in, under $26 million traded. That is the AG's core case that NovaTech's promised trading profits were not real.
  • A $1.37 billion judgment is not $1.37 billion recovered. The Petions left the country, and neither the proposed order nor the release says how the AG will collect or pay victims, so the money investors get back could be far smaller.
  • The 2% to 4% weekly promise works out to more than 100% a year. Returns that high, plus bonuses for bringing in friends, are the warning signs this case shows regulators look for.

What's next

  • Any collection steps, asset freezes or a claims process for NovaTech investors from the AG.
  • Whether federal or foreign authorities act on the Petions, who left the U.S.
  • Rulings on AWS Mining's other promoters.
