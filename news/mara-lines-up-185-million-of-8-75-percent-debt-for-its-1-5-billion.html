MARA lines up $185 million of 8.75% debt for its $1.5 billion power deal

Two SEC filings this week show MARA priced $185 million of 8.75% notes to help buy Long Ridge Energy & Power. Neither filing mentions bitcoin, weeks after the miner sold 13,597 coins to repay loans.

LC Insight Editorial Team3 min read

Key points

  • On November 17, a MARA subsidiary priced $185 million of 8.750% senior secured notes due 2032 at 99% of face value, a filing with the SEC shows.
  • The notes are issued by Long Ridge Energy LLC and will help pay for MARA's roughly $1.5 billion purchase of its parent, Long Ridge Energy & Power. Closing is expected on November 27, 2026.
  • Neither of this week's two filings mentions bitcoin. MARA's earlier sale of 13,597 bitcoin for about $1.1 billion went mostly to repay bitcoin-backed loans.

What MARA filed

Bitcoin miner MARA Holdings filed two short reports with the SEC this week. The first, dated November 16, said its subsidiary MARA USA Corporation had started an offering of $185 million of 8.750% senior secured notes due 2032, issued by Long Ridge. "Senior secured" means the lenders are first in line and backed by assets.

The second, dated November 17, says the notes were priced at 99.000% of face value, plus interest accrued since August 15, 2026. They are sold privately to large institutional buyers, not to the public.

Who borrows, and why

The borrower is not MARA itself. The notes are issued by Long Ridge Energy LLC, and they add to $600 million of the same notes that Long Ridge already issued under a February 19, 2025, agreement with its lenders.

MARA agreed on April 29, 2026, to buy 100% of Long Ridge Energy & Power LLC, the parent of the borrower, for a base price of about $1.5 billion. The sellers are Ohio River Partners Holdco, Ohio River Partners Finance and FTAI Infrastructure. The filings say the money from the notes, together with MARA's cash, will pay for the purchase, repay part of Long Ridge's existing debt and cover fees.

The offering depends on the deal closing. The November 17 filing says both are expected to close on November 27, 2026.

What the notes raise

At 99% of face value, the $185 million of notes bring in about $183.2 million before accrued interest and fees. That is our own math from the price in the filing. Once issued, Long Ridge would owe $785 million on this series of notes.

Where bitcoin fits

Neither filing mentions bitcoin, as collateral, as a sale or as a holding. The bitcoin sales came earlier. MARA's third-quarter shareholder letter says that after September 30 it sold 13,597 bitcoin at an average of about $81,039, for about $1.1 billion, and used $750 million to fully repay its bitcoin-backed loans.

Bitcoin traded at $63,940 at 6:35 a.m. ET on Thursday, November 19, down 13.1% in seven days, according to CoinGecko.

LC Insight analysis

This section is our reading of the facts above, not a statement of fact.

  • We think the filings show MARA paying for its energy push with Long Ridge's own debt rather than more bitcoin sales. Borrowing at the subsidiary keeps the new debt tied to the power business.
  • An 8.75% coupon priced below face value is a costly loan. The likely result is that Long Ridge's power sales, not bitcoin's price, will need to carry these interest payments.
  • November 27 is the date to mark. The offering depends on the acquisition closing, so if the deal slips, the new debt slips with it.

What's next

  • Whether the acquisition and the notes close on November 27, 2026, as the filing expects.
  • Any new MARA filing on bitcoin sales or holdings after the deal closes.
