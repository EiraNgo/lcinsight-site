Bitcoin has never lost two years in a row. It needs a 34% jump in 46 days to avoid it

Bitcoin closed 2025 at $87,509 on CoinMarketCap, down 6.3% for the year. At about $65,500 on Sunday, November 15, 2026, it is down 25% in 2026. CoinMarketCap's daily record since 2013 shows every losing year followed by a gain.

LC Insight Editorial Team3 min read

Key points

  • Bitcoin's 2025 close on CoinMarketCap was about $87,509, down 6.3% from $93,429 at the end of 2024.
  • At about $65,508 on CoinGecko at 6:44 a.m. ET November 15, 2026, bitcoin is down about 25% this year and needs a 34% gain by December 31 to finish higher.
  • In CoinMarketCap's data back to 2013, bitcoin's three earlier losing years, 2014, 2018 and 2022, were each followed by a gain.

Where bitcoin stands

Bitcoin traded at about $65,508 at 6:44 a.m. ET on Sunday, November 15, 2026, according to CoinGecko. It ended 2025 at about $87,509, the daily close on December 31 in CoinMarketCap's historical data.

That leaves bitcoin about 25% down for 2026 with 46 days to go, counting November 16 through December 31. To finish the year above its 2025 close, it has to rise about 34% from Sunday morning's price.

The year 2025 was itself a losing year. Bitcoin closed 2024 at about $93,429, so it fell 6.3% in 2025, even though it set its record high of $126,080 on October 6, 2025, per CoinGecko.

Every year since 2013

We checked the December 31 close for every year in CoinMarketCap's daily data. Bitcoin had three earlier losing years: 2014 (down 57.5%), 2018 (down 73.6%) and 2022 (down 64.3%). Each was followed by a gain the next year: 34.5% in 2015, 92.2% in 2019 and 155.4% in 2023.

So in this record, a second losing year in a row would be a first. The record has limits. It covers 12 full calendar years of change, 2014 through 2025, and past patterns do not set future prices.

How 2026 got here

Bitcoin's highest daily close of 2026 on CoinMarketCap was about $96,929 on January 14. The lowest was about $58,559 on June 30. It closed at $84,012 on October 21 and has fallen about 22% since then.

The last week did much of the damage. Bitcoin closed at $74,585 on November 8 and at $64,128 on November 14, a drop of about 14% in six days.

What would have to happen

Bitcoin would have to climb from about $65,500 to above $87,509 in six and a half weeks. For scale, it moved about 22% the other way in the less than four weeks since October 21.

We are not predicting either outcome. The year-end close is a date on a calendar, and nothing in the market resets on it. It is still the number most yearly charts, fund reports and headlines will use.

LC Insight analysis

This section is our reading of the facts above, not a statement of fact.

  • We think the two-losing-years line will shape coverage more than trading. Fund managers report calendar-year returns, so a second down year for bitcoin would show up in every annual summary.
  • The likely risk for holders is not the date itself but what drove 2026 lower: we will watch whether the selling slows before year end.
  • Each past rebound followed a loss of 57% or more. A 6.3% loss in 2025 is a much milder case, so we think the old pattern says little about what comes next.

What's next

  • Bitcoin against its 2025 close of about $87,509 by December 31
  • The weekly close on Sunday, November 15
  • Whether the 2026 low close of about $58,559 from June 30 is tested
