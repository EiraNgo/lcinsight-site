US prices rose 0.4% in October, with no yearly inflation rate. Bitcoin fell 1.4% on the day

October's consumer price index, out November 10, slowed from September's 0.6% jump. The Labor Department could not publish the usual 12-month rate because it collected no data in October 2025. Bitcoin dipped about 0.6% in 20 minutes around the release and ended the day near $73,650.

LC Insight Editorial Team3 min read

Key points

  • The consumer price index rose 0.4% in October after seasonal adjustment, down from 0.6% in September. Core prices, without food and energy, rose 0.3% for a third month.
  • There is no 12-month headline rate this time: the Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) did not collect October 2025 data because of a lapse in government funding. September's yearly rate was 3.7%.
  • Bitcoin fell from about $74,240 at 8:25 a.m. ET to $73,795 at 8:45 a.m., CoinGecko data show, and was near $73,650 at midnight, down about 1.4% on the day.

A smaller rise than September

US consumer prices rose 0.4% in October after seasonal adjustment, the Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) said at 8:30 a.m. ET on Tuesday, November 10, 2026. In September they rose 0.6%, the September release shows.

Core CPI leaves out food and energy because they swing a lot. It rose 0.3% in October, the same as in August and September.

The big change was fuel. Energy prices rose 0.7% in October after jumping 5.2% in September. Gasoline rose 1.0% after 9.1%.

Why there is no yearly number

Most people know inflation as a yearly rate, such as September's 3.7%. This month that headline is missing. The release says BLS "did not collect survey data for October 2025" because of a lapse in appropriations, meaning a gap in government funding. Without October 2025 prices, BLS says it cannot publish 12-month changes for indexes where that data is missing.

Its summary table still lists a few yearly figures, before seasonal adjustment: gasoline up 37.4% and used cars and trucks down 3.3%.

What rose and what fell

Food prices turned up. They rose 0.5% in October after no change in September, and food at home rose 0.4% after a 0.1% dip. Electricity rose 0.9%, while piped gas for homes fell 0.8%.

Shelter, the largest part of the basket, rose 0.3%, a little slower than 0.4% in September. Recreation rose 0.8% and car insurance 0.7%. Used cars and trucks fell 1.2%, and communication slipped 0.1%.

Other prices moved less. Medical care rose 0.3%, new vehicles 0.2% and apparel 0.1%, the release shows. Before seasonal adjustment, the all-items index stood at 337.511 (1982 to 1984 prices = 100), up 0.2% from September. Wage earners' prices, measured by the CPI-W, rose 0.1% on the same basis.

How bitcoin moved

Bitcoin slipped after the release (new to bitcoin? Read our guide). CoinGecko five-minute data puts it at about $74,240 at 8:25 a.m. ET and $73,795 at 8:45 a.m., a drop of about 0.6%. It then climbed back to about $74,560 by 10:05 a.m.

The gain did not last. Bitcoin fell to about $72,960 by 10:45 a.m. and touched about $72,840 at noon in CoinGecko's hourly data. CoinGecko's price data does not show why it fell, and we do not guess. At midnight it was near $73,650, about 1.4% below its price at the start of Tuesday. CoinGecko showed about $73,765 early on November 11, down 1.2% in 24 hours.

What comes next

The next CPI report, for November, is due Thursday, December 10, 2026, at 8:30 a.m. ET, BLS says.

LC Insight analysis

This section is our reading of the facts above, not a statement of fact.

  • We think a 0.4% monthly rise is still fast: kept up for a year it would mean prices nearly 5% higher. The slowdown from September came mostly from fuel, which can swing back. This is not investment advice.
  • The missing yearly number makes this report harder to read at a glance. The likely result is more attention on the monthly core figure, which has sat at 0.3% for three months.
