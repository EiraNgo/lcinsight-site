PEPE fell 11% in a day. Shorts now pay longs on half of its $465 million in futures bets

The frog memecoin was the worst performer among the 50 biggest coins on CoinGecko. Its open futures bets equal about a quarter of its market value, and on the biggest market, Binance's, shorts now pay longs.

LC Insight Editorial Team3 min read

Key points

  • PEPE traded at $0.00000445 at 6:49 a.m. ET on November 12, 2026, down 10.8% in 24 hours, the biggest drop in CoinGecko's top 50.
  • At 7:00 a.m. ET, 80 PEPE perpetual futures on CoinGecko's list held $465.4 million of open bets, about 25% of PEPE's $1.87 billion market value, and traded $2.20 billion in a day.
  • Funding was negative on 27 of the 80. Those 27, led by Binance's $94.9 million market, hold $232.2 million, or 50%, of all open PEPE bets.

What moved

PEPE, a memecoin built on Ethereum, traded at $0.00000445 at 6:49 a.m. ET on November 12, 2026, per CoinGecko. It was down 10.8% in 24 hours and 10.3% in seven days. No coin in CoinGecko's top 50 by market value fell more over the day. Bitcoin was down about 1.5% and ether about 1.8%.

The drop came in steps. CoinGecko's hourly prices show PEPE near $0.00000555 at 10 a.m. ET on November 10. It slid through Wednesday, from about $0.00000499 at 7 a.m. ET to $0.00000452 at 7 p.m. ET, and touched about $0.00000440 around 6 a.m. ET on Thursday. Spot trading in the 24 hours to 7:00 a.m. ET came to $481.3 million.

Where the bets sit

A perpetual future is a bet on a price that never expires. Open interest counts the dollars in bets nobody has closed yet. At 7:00 a.m. ET, CoinGecko's derivatives list showed 80 PEPE perpetuals (some count 1,000 PEPE as one unit) holding $465.4 million. They traded $2.20 billion in a day, about 4.6 times PEPE's spot volume.

Binance's 1000PEPEUSDT market was the biggest, with $94.9 million open and $442.4 million traded. MEXC followed with $70.2 million, then Gate with $43.1 million and OKX with $36.6 million. On Hyperliquid, an onchain futures exchange, the kPEPE market held $22.6 million and traded $46.5 million.

Who pays whom

Funding is a small fee that passes between the two sides of a perpetual to keep its price near the coin's spot price. When it is negative, traders betting on a fall (shorts) pay traders betting on a rise (longs).

Funding was negative on 27 of 80 PEPE perpetuals, positive on 49 and zero on 4. The negative markets include the two largest: they hold $232.2 million, or 50% of all open PEPE bets. Binance stood at minus 0.0055% and MEXC at minus 0.0061%. Bybit, Bitget and Deepcoin stayed positive, and Hyperliquid's kPEPE rate was plus 0.00125% per hour.

The public data we read show the size of the bets and the fees, not who holds them or whether any were force-closed.

LC Insight analysis

This section is our reading of the facts above, not a statement of fact.

  • We think the split matters more than the total: on Binance and MEXC, the two biggest PEPE markets, shorts are now paying, while smaller venues still have longs paying.
  • Open bets near a quarter of PEPE's market value mean futures can push the price harder than spot buyers and sellers alone. That works in both directions.
  • Negative funding costs shorts money every few hours. If the slide stalls, some may close, which means buying PEPE back.
