Rumor vs record: bitcoin at $61,768 is still above MARA's $39,674 energy cost per coin

As bitcoin slid under $62,000, posts claimed it now costs more to mine a coin than it sells for and that miners are dumping. We checked five claims against price data and a large U.S. listed miner's own filings.

LC Insight Editorial Team4 min read

Key points

  • Bitcoin traded at $61,768 at 6:44 a.m. ET on Friday, November 20, 2026, down 3.4% in 24 hours, CoinGecko data show.
  • MARA Holdings, a large U.S. listed miner, paid $39,674 in purchased energy per bitcoin at its own sites in the third quarter. Its letter gives no all-in cost per coin.
  • MARA sold 3,891 bitcoin in the quarter while mining 2,247, and bought none. That is one company's record through September 30, not the whole industry.

Why we did this check

Bitcoin kept falling overnight and traded at $61,768 at 6:44 a.m. ET on Friday, November 20, 2026, according to CoinGecko. As it slipped under $62,000, posts spread claiming the price had fallen below what it costs miners to produce a coin, and that miners were selling at a record pace.

Miners are the companies that run the computers securing the bitcoin network and earn new coins for it. If the price falls under their costs, some may sell coins or switch off machines, so the claim matters. We checked it against the latest filings of MARA Holdings, a large U.S. listed miner. The table above gives each verdict.

What MARA's own numbers say

MARA's third-quarter shareholder letter, filed with the SEC on November 9, 2026, shows it mined 2,247 bitcoin in the three months to September 30, about 24.4 a day. It paid $39,674 in purchased energy per bitcoin at its own sites, up slightly from $39,235 a year earlier.

Energy is the biggest running cost, but not the only one. The letter does not turn depreciation, staff or hosting into a cost per coin, so no single "cost to mine" figure comes from MARA itself.

MARA's letter puts the average bitcoin price for the coins it produced in the quarter at $71,688. Friday's price is about 14% below that.

Selling to build cash

MARA has been a seller. It sold 3,891 bitcoin in the third quarter, about 1.7 times what it mined, at an average of $75,300, and bought none. The letter says the company intends to keep using bitcoin to build cash, for example for AI computing projects.

Its 10-Q quarterly report shows $1.92 billion of proceeds from selling digital assets in the first nine months of 2026, more than ten times the $182.4 million of a year earlier. At September 30, MARA counted 34,012 bitcoin in total, including 17,852 pledged as collateral for loans and 1,408 lent out.

What we could not check

MARA's figures stop at September 30, before November's slide from about $77,000 to about $61,000. We could not load public network hashrate data on Friday, so we cannot say whether miners as a group are switching machines off this week. One company's sales also cannot show that miners overall are selling "like never before".

LC Insight analysis

This section is our reading of the facts above, not a statement of fact.

  • We think the cost claim is too simple. At MARA's energy cost, mining still pays at $61,768. MARA gives no all-in cost per coin, so whether it still pays after machines and debt cannot be checked from its filings.
  • MARA's selling looks like a business choice, raising cash for AI projects and debt, rather than forced dumping. A falling price would make each coin sold raise less.
  • The 17,852 bitcoin pledged as collateral matter more if prices keep falling, because lenders can ask for more collateral.

What's next

  • MARA's next filings, for any bitcoin sold in October and November.
  • Bitcoin's next mining difficulty change, which shows whether computing power is leaving the network.
  • Whether other listed miners report selling more than they mine.
