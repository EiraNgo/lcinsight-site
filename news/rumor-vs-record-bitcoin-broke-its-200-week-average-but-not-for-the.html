Rumor vs record: bitcoin broke its 200-week average, but not for the first time

After bitcoin's weekend slide, posts claimed a first-ever break of the 200-week average, a record fear reading and an 18% loss for Strategy. We checked five claims against CoinMarketCap, CoinGecko and Strategy's own filing.

LC Insight Editorial Team3 min read

Key points

  • Bitcoin closed Saturday, November 14, 2026, at about $64,128 on CoinMarketCap, below its 200-week average of about $67,750 by our count.
  • It is not the first break: bitcoin closed below that average on 24 days between June 5 and August 16, 2026.
  • The total crypto market lost about $377 billion in the seven days to 7 p.m. ET Saturday, per CoinMarketCap.
  • Strategy was about 13% below its $75,456 average cost at 6:44 a.m. ET Sunday, and about 18% below only at Saturday's low.

Why we did this check

Bitcoin fell about 13.6% in seven days to about $65,508 at 6:44 a.m. ET on Sunday, November 15, 2026, according to CoinGecko. Its 24-hour range ran from $62,081 to $67,253.

As the slide deepened, a new set of posts spread. They were about a long-term trend line, the size of the market's loss, fear readings and Strategy, the largest corporate bitcoin holder. We checked five claims that our earlier check did not cover. We used CoinMarketCap, CoinGecko and Strategy's own SEC filing only.

The 200-week line

The 200-week moving average is the mean of bitcoin's last 200 weekly closing prices, almost four years of data. Many traders treat it as a floor in bear markets, which is why a break gets attention.

We built it from CoinMarketCap's daily closes, using the Sunday close of each week from January 15, 2023, through November 8, 2026. That gives about $67,750. Bitcoin closed at $68,249 on Friday, November 13, just above the line, then at $64,128 on Saturday, about 5% below it.

The claim that this never happened before does not hold up. The same calculation shows bitcoin closing below the average on 24 days between June 5 and August 16, 2026, the last at about $62,819 against a line near $63,776. Other sites can show a slightly different number for the line, because some use a different weekly close time.

The $377 billion week

CoinMarketCap takes a snapshot of the total crypto market value each day at midnight UTC, which is 7 p.m. ET. On its chart, the figure fell from about $2.541 trillion on November 7 to about $2.164 trillion on November 14.

That is a loss of about $377 billion in seven days, so the claim of more than $300 billion is right. The biggest single step was the last one: about $138 billion between Friday and Saturday evening.

Strategy's gap and the fear gauge

Strategy's Form 8-K filed November 9 lists 850,364 bitcoin bought for an average of $75,456 each, about $64.16 billion in total. At $65,508, those coins were worth about $55.7 billion, roughly 13% less than they cost. The 18% figure only applies at Saturday's low of $62,081. A paper loss does not by itself force Strategy to sell.

On CoinMarketCap's Fear and Greed Index, which scores sentiment from 0 to 100, the reading fell from 22 on November 12 to 13 on November 15. That is extreme fear, but not a record. The index hit 5 on February 6, 2026.

LC Insight analysis

This section is our reading of the facts above, not a statement of fact.

  • We think the 200-week break matters less as a signal than as a test. Bitcoin spent parts of June to August 2026 below the line before climbing back, and the coming weekly closes will show whether this break lasts.
  • The likely reason posts overstate Strategy's loss is that they use the intraday low. A price at one moment is not the same as where the market settles.
  • Of the five claims, two were wrong and one only partly true. Posts that say "first time" or "record" were the ones that failed the check.

What's next

  • Sunday's weekly close against the 200-week average near $67,750
  • Strategy's next weekly 8-K filing, usually on Monday
  • Whether CoinMarketCap's fear reading stays in extreme fear
