Bitcoin ETFs bled $1.72 billion over seven straight outflow days as bitcoin slid to $65,000

U.S. spot bitcoin funds had a net outflow of $185.4 million on Friday, CoinMarketCap data show, and Monday's figure was revised to a $9.6 million inflow. The seven-day run is their longest in the 63 sessions CoinMarketCap's 90-day chart covers.

LC Insight Editorial Team3 min read

Key points

  • U.S. spot bitcoin ETFs had a net outflow of $185.4 million on Friday, November 13, 2026, CoinMarketCap data show. Monday, November 16, first listed as a $66.9 million outflow, was revised to a $9.6 million inflow.
  • That makes seven outflow sessions in a row, November 5 to November 13, totaling $1.72 billion. It is the longest run in the 63 sessions CoinMarketCap's 90-day chart covers.
  • Ether funds were almost flat: plus $2.3 million on Friday and minus $8.4 million on Monday.

Seven days of selling

Spot bitcoin ETFs are listed funds, bought and sold like shares on U.S. exchanges, that hold actual bitcoin. When more money leaves than comes in (an outflow), the fund usually has to sell or hand back bitcoin.

According to CoinMarketCap's ETF flow data, U.S. spot bitcoin funds lost a net $185.4 million on Friday, November 13. Monday, November 16, now shows a net inflow of $9.6 million. The last inflow day was Wednesday, November 4, when $75.2 million came in.

From November 5 to November 13, money left on seven straight trading days, a total of $1.72 billion. The worst day of the run was Thursday, November 5, with $430.2 million out. CoinMarketCap's 90-day chart, which covers 63 sessions back to late August, shows no longer outflow run.

Correction, November 18, 2026: CoinMarketCap revised Monday, November 16, from a $66.9 million outflow to a $9.6 million inflow, so the outflow run lasted seven days and totaled $1.72 billion, not eight days and $1.79 billion.

Smaller outflows, lower price

The smallest outflow of the run was $51.0 million on November 12. After the run, Monday brought a small inflow. The selling slowed while the price kept falling.

CoinMarketCap lists bitcoin at about $77,338 beside the November 4 flow and about $65,315 beside Monday's, a drop of about 16%.

In November through Monday, bitcoin funds have lost $2.16 billion in 11 sessions, with two inflow days.

Ether funds go quiet

U.S. spot ether ETFs took in $2.3 million on Friday and lost $8.4 million on Monday, CoinMarketCap shows. Both days were small next to the $139.5 million that left on November 11. Ether funds have lost $640.8 million in November.

At 12:41 a.m. ET on Tuesday, November 17, CoinGecko showed bitcoin at $64,879, up 0.3% in 24 hours, and ether at $1,836.18, up 1.2%.

What we could not confirm

CoinMarketCap gives one total per day for all funds, not fund by fund, so we cannot say which fund lost the most on Friday or Monday. The daily figures show the dollar value of the flows, not how much bitcoin left the funds, so we do not convert them into coins.

LC Insight analysis

This section is our reading of the facts above, not a statement of fact.

  • We think the shrinking daily outflows matter more than the streak itself. Sellers left in size on November 5 and 6. Friday was a fraction of that, and Monday turned to a small inflow.
  • Ether funds are close to zero flow for two sessions after weeks of steady selling. That looks more like buyers stepping back than a new wave of selling.
  • With Monday revised to an inflow, the run stopped at seven days, while the price fell about 16% from November 4 to Monday.

What's next

  • Tuesday, November 17: whether bitcoin funds post a second straight inflow.
  • Whether November's $2.16 billion outflow keeps growing as the month ends.
  • Fund-by-fund numbers from issuer pages such as iShares.
