Bullpen breach: $2.57 million left old beta accounts and now sits as 42 BTC

An on-chain trace shows the funds moving from Solana to Arbitrum and then into four Bitcoin addresses, where they have not moved. Bullpen says it took a test service offline and only early beta accounts were hit.

LC Insight Editorial Team3 min read

Key points

  • An Arbitrum address used by the attacker received $2,565,767 in USDC in 28 payments by 5:33 a.m. ET on November 24, Bitquery's on-chain investigation shows.
  • Starting at 12:49 p.m. ET that day, the funds were swapped through Bridgers into 42.24 BTC across four Bitcoin addresses. Bitquery saw none of it move as of 9:40 a.m. ET on November 25.
  • Bullpen said 18 to 19 accounts from its April to June 2024 beta lost funds and that it took a test service offline, according to Bitquery's account of the company's post on X.

What happened

Funds started leaving a Solana wallet tied to an early Bullpen beta account at 1:46 a.m. ET on Tuesday, November 24, according to Bitquery's on-chain investigation. Bullpen is a crypto trading app. The wallet belongs to the well-known trader who posts as Ansem, who said on X that someone got into his Bullpen profile, where he had imported his Solana private key.

Bullpen later said 18 to 19 early accounts, created during its beta between April and June 2024, lost funds. It said it took an affected test service offline and would contact users directly, according to Bitquery's summary of the company's post. Bullpen has not published a technical cause that we could open, and no official body has named the attacker.

Where the money went

The trace shows 29,104 SOL plus about $515,000 in USDC sent into the Relay bridge on Solana, then out on Arbitrum. There, a single address (0xAC33...1b4d) collected $2,565,767 in USDC by 5:33 a.m. ET. Bitquery also counts about $2.1 million of memecoins sold from the wallet between 3:30 a.m. and 6 a.m. ET, mostly in Pumpswap and Meteora pools.

From 12:49 p.m. ET, the USDC was swapped through Bridgers into Bitcoin: 42.24 BTC, worth about $2.57 million at CoinGecko's price of $60,862 at 12:45 a.m. ET on Thursday. Early on November 25, another wallet of unknown ownership sent $1.58 million in USDC to the same address on Ethereum, which was swapped for 951 ETH. Bitquery says the chain cannot show whether that owner agreed to the transfer.

What happened to the ANSEM token

The Solana memecoin ANSEM (listed as The Black Bull on CoinGecko) traded at $0.0369 at 1 a.m. ET on November 24 and $0.0206 at 7 a.m. ET, a 44% drop in CoinGecko's hourly data. Its 24-hour trading volume jumped from about $0.9 million to $17.2 million in the same window. At 12:45 a.m. ET on Thursday it was still at $0.0206, down 8.4% in a day, with a market value of $9.2 million.

Other accounts swept

Bitquery found more accounts swept to the same thief address: six Hyperliquid accounts that lost a combined $659, seven Polygon accounts and a Polymarket account that lost 364 pUSD. That makes 14 other accounts in its count, below Bullpen's 18 to 19. Bitquery says the rest may have held nothing or held coins on chains it did not check. A Hyperliquid account linked to the main victim was not touched.

LC Insight analysis

This section is our reading of the facts above, not a statement of fact.

  • We think the key fact for users is the age of the accounts: every hit Bullpen described came from its 2024 beta, which points to an old test system rather than the main app. That reading rests on Bullpen's own statement until it publishes a full report.
  • The 42.24 BTC is sitting still in four known addresses. The first move out of them will show where the attacker tries to cash out, and exchanges can watch for it.
  • The hack also hit a token. ANSEM fell 44% in six hours on November 24 as its trading volume jumped about 19 times, and it was still at that low on Thursday.

What's next

  • A full incident report from Bullpen with a cause and a refund plan.
  • Any movement from the four Bitcoin addresses or the three ETH holding wallets.
  • Whether more early beta users come forward.
