MiCA CASP license: services, capital, deadlines and the EU register

A MiCA CASP license is the EU authorization a crypto firm needs to serve European clients. A national regulator grants it, the firm must hold EUR 50,000 to EUR 150,000 of minimum capital depending on its services, and one license can be passported across the EU.

LC Insight Editorial Team7 min read

Key points

  • A CASP (crypto-asset service provider) needs authorization from a national regulator in the EU country where it has its registered office.
  • Minimum capital under MiCA Annex IV is EUR 50,000, EUR 125,000 or EUR 150,000, depending on the services offered, or a quarter of last year's fixed overheads if that is higher.
  • Regulators have 25 working days to check an application is complete and 40 working days to decide once it is.
  • The grandfathering period for firms licensed under old national rules ended on July 1, 2026, at the latest.
  • ESMA's interim MiCA register lists authorized CASPs and non-compliant entities, updated weekly.

What is a MiCA CASP license?

MiCA, the Markets in Crypto-Assets Regulation, is the European Union's crypto law. Its formal name is Regulation (EU) 2023/1114. Because it is a regulation, the same text applies directly in every EU country.

A CASP, or crypto-asset service provider, is a firm that offers crypto services to clients as a business. Under Article 59 of MiCA, a firm that wants to do this must be a legal person (a company, not an individual) authorized under Article 63. It needs a registered office in an EU country where it carries out at least part of its services, and at least one of its directors must live in the EU.

The license is granted by a national regulator, called a national competent authority, not by an EU agency. This guide is general information, not legal advice.

Which crypto services need a CASP license?

MiCA covers ten crypto services. Annex IV of the regulation, which sets capital by service, names all of them:

  • Executing orders for crypto on behalf of clients
  • Placing crypto-assets (helping an issuer sell tokens)
  • Transfer services for crypto on behalf of clients
  • Receiving and transmitting orders for crypto
  • Giving advice on crypto-assets
  • Portfolio management of crypto-assets
  • Custody and administration of crypto for clients
  • Exchanging crypto for funds, such as euros
  • Exchanging crypto for other crypto
  • Operating a crypto-asset trading platform

A typical crypto exchange runs a trading platform, holds client coins and swaps crypto for cash, so it touches several of these at once. A wallet app that only moves coins for users may need a license for transfer services alone.

How much capital does a CASP need?

Article 67 says a CASP must at all times hold prudential safeguards equal to at least the higher of two numbers: the permanent minimum capital in Annex IV for its services, or one quarter of its fixed overheads for the preceding year, reviewed every year.

Annex IV sets three classes, per the regulation text:

  • Class 1, EUR 50,000: order execution, placing, transfer services, reception and transmission of orders, advice and portfolio management.
  • Class 2, EUR 125,000: all Class 1 services plus custody and administration, and exchanging crypto for funds or for other crypto.
  • Class 3, EUR 150,000: all Class 2 services plus operating a trading platform.

For a large exchange, the overheads test usually matters more. A firm with EUR 10 million of yearly fixed costs, for example, would need EUR 2.5 million, far above the Class 3 floor.

How do you apply for a CASP license?

The firm files with the regulator in its home EU country. Article 62 lists what the application must contain, in points (a) to (s), starting with the firm's legal name and any trading names. The list also covers the business plan, governance, the people who run and own the firm, and the services it plans to offer.

Article 63 then sets the clock, all in working days:

  • Within 5 working days, the regulator acknowledges receipt.
  • Within 25 working days, it checks whether the application is complete and asks for anything missing.
  • Within 40 working days of a complete application, it decides whether to grant or refuse the license.
  • It can ask for more information no later than the 20th working day of that period, which pauses the clock for up to 20 working days.
  • It tells the applicant its decision within 5 working days of making it.

Refusals must rest on objective and demonstrable grounds. In practice, preparing the file usually takes far longer than the legal review.

The clock only starts once the file is complete, so a thin application can sit in the completeness stage for a long time. Firms that offer several services should apply for all of them at once, because the capital class and the review both depend on the full list of services.

How does MiCA passporting work?

Passporting means one license works in all EU countries. Under Article 65, a CASP that wants to serve clients in other member states sends its home regulator a list of those countries, the services it will offer there and a start date.

The home regulator has 10 working days to forward that information to the other countries, ESMA and the EBA (the European Banking Authority), and must tell the firm when it has done so. The firm can start from the moment it receives that message, or at the latest from the 15th calendar day after it sent its information. No second license is needed.

This is the main commercial reason firms want a MiCA license: one approval opens a market of 27 countries.

When did the MiCA transitional period end?

Firms that were already serving clients under national law before December 30, 2024, could keep going under a grandfathering clause in Article 143(3). That window ran until July 1, 2026, or until the firm's MiCA license was granted or refused, whichever came first, ESMA says. EU countries could choose shorter periods, and ESMA publishes the list of national choices.

The result is simple: since July 1, 2026, a firm without a MiCA license should not be serving EU clients. ESMA has also pushed licensed firms to drop stablecoins that do not meet MiCA, as LC Insight reported in our story on its October opinion.

Where can you check if a firm has a MiCA license?

ESMA runs an interim MiCA register made of five CSV files: white papers for ordinary crypto-assets, issuers of asset-referenced tokens, issuers of e-money tokens, authorized CASPs, and non-compliant entities providing crypto services. ESMA updates it weekly, and the latest version is dated November 11, 2026, per its MiCA page.

Two cautions. Data from national regulators may not show up immediately, so the regulator's own register is the final word. And withdrawn licenses stay in the file with the withdrawal date, so read that column before treating a firm as licensed.

Stablecoin issuers sit under separate rules. The EBA supervises the largest ones directly, starting with USDC, as we explained in our USDC story.

How is a MiCA license different from Dubai or Singapore rules?

MiCA is one rulebook for 27 countries with fixed capital floors and fixed review deadlines written into law. Dubai's VARA and Singapore's MAS run their own licensing regimes, each with its own rules on who needs a license and what it allows. LC Insight has separate guides to the Dubai VARA license and the Singapore MAS DPT license, and a side-by-side guide to crypto exchange licenses by country.

One point applies everywhere: a license from one place does not let a firm serve clients in another, except inside the EU through the MiCA passport.

LC Insight analysis

This section is our reading of the facts above, not a statement of fact.

  • We think the overheads test, not the EUR 150,000 floor, is what decides capital for any exchange of real size. Readers who see a small capital figure quoted for a big platform should treat it with caution.
  • The end of grandfathering turns ESMA's register into the key document for EU users. The non-compliant entities file is as useful as the authorized list.
  • Passporting makes the choice of home regulator a strategic decision. Watch whether ESMA's push for consistent supervision narrows the gaps between national regulators.

Frequently asked questions

How long does a MiCA CASP license take?

By law, the regulator has 25 working days to confirm the file is complete and 40 working days to decide after that, with up to 20 more working days if it asks questions. Preparing the application often takes much longer.

What is the minimum capital for a MiCA license?

EUR 50,000, EUR 125,000 or EUR 150,000 depending on the services, under Annex IV. If a quarter of last year's fixed overheads is higher, that amount applies instead.

Can a non-EU company get a MiCA license?

It must set up a legal person with a registered office in an EU country where it provides part of its services, and at least one director must live in the EU.

Does a MiCA license work in every EU country?

Yes, through passporting under Article 65. The firm notifies its home regulator, which forwards the details, and it can start within 15 calendar days at the latest.

Is ESMA's register the official list?

It is an interim register built from national regulators' data and updated weekly. ESMA notes that new data may not appear immediately, so the national regulator's register is the best final check.
