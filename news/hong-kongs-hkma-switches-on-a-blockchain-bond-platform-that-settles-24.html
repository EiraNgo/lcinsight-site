Hong Kong's HKMA switches on a blockchain bond platform that settles 24 hours a day

The Hong Kong Monetary Authority launched the CMU Digital Asset Platform on November 25, 2026. It moves bonds from issuance to redemption on a shared ledger, settles in multiple currencies around the clock, and is meant to link up with regulated stablecoins later.

LC Insight Editorial Team3 min read

Key points

  • The HKMA announced the official launch of the CMU Digital Asset Platform (CMU DAP) on November 25, 2026. It is built on distributed ledger technology and run by CMU OmniClear for the HKMA.
  • The platform handles bonds paperlessly from first issuance to redemption, settles on-chain with atomic delivery versus payment, and supports 24-hour settlement in multiple currencies using cash tokens.
  • Next, CMU OmniClear plans to explore funds and other real-world assets and to connect the platform with tokenized deposits and regulated stablecoins.

What happened

The Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA), the city's de facto central bank, announced the official launch of the CMU Digital Asset Platform on November 25, 2026. CMU stands for Central Moneymarkets Unit, the system that already clears and settles Hong Kong dollar bonds and other debt securities. The new platform runs on distributed ledger technology (DLT), a shared database of the kind blockchains use.

CMU OmniClear Limited built the platform and operates it on the HKMA's behalf. The HKMA called the launch a major milestone and said the goal is faster settlement and lower operating costs.

What the platform does

According to the HKMA, the platform offers:

  • A paperless process for the whole life of a bond: first issuance, clearing and settlement of trades, coupon payments and redemption at maturity.
  • Tokenization of traditional debt securities, meaning ordinary bonds can also be recorded as tokens on the ledger.
  • Atomic delivery versus payment (DvP) on-chain. In plain words: the bond and the cash change hands in one step, or neither moves, so neither side is left exposed.
  • Direct links to Real Time Gross Settlement (RTGS) systems, the payment rails banks use with the central bank, and to central bank digital currencies (CBDC).
  • 24-hour settlement in multiple currencies using cash tokens.

The HKMA did not publish trade volumes, a participant list or the names of any bonds issued on the platform so far.

Why crypto watchers care

The release sets out a roadmap that points at the crypto world. CMU OmniClear, working with market participants, will explore expanding the platform from debt securities to funds and real-world assets, and integrating it with other DLT platforms and payment instruments, including tokenized deposits and regulated stablecoins, the HKMA said.

HKMA Chief Executive Eddie Yue, who also chairs the board of CMU OmniClear Holdings, described the platform as core infrastructure for a multi-asset digital financial ecosystem.

The launch follows the HKMA's move on November 2 to put EnsembleTX, its settlement platform for tokenized transactions, on a 24/7 footing with real money, which we covered in our story on Hong Kong's 24/7 tokenized funds.

What we do not know yet

The release does not name the banks or issuers using the platform, the ledger technology underneath, or a date for the stablecoin link. It also gives no figures. We will update this story when CMU OmniClear or the HKMA publishes them.

LC Insight analysis

This section is our reading of the facts above, not a statement of fact.

  • We think the important part is the plumbing, not the word blockchain: a central bank's own bond system now settles on a ledger around the clock, which is the setting tokenized assets need to trade outside office hours.
  • The stablecoin line is a plan, not a product. Until a regulated stablecoin actually settles a bond trade on CMU DAP, it stays a stated goal.

What's next

  • The first named bond issues and banks on CMU DAP
  • A date for linking the platform to tokenized deposits and regulated stablecoins
  • Any extension from bonds to tokenized funds
