Rumor vs record: did whales really load up on XRP as it slid to $1.10?

XRP fell 5.4% in a day and hit its lowest price since August. Posts say whales are buying the dip. We checked six viral claims. The price claims mostly hold up; the whale claims cannot be confirmed yet.

LC Insight Editorial Team3 min read

Key points

  • CoinGecko shows XRP at about $1.10 at 12:40 a.m. ET on November 9, 2026, down 5.4% in 24 hours, 12.3% in seven days and 70% below its July 2025 record of $3.65.
  • The claim of a 14% drop from $1.34 is close but high. CoinGecko's hourly data peaks at $1.32 on November 5, so the slide to $1.15 on November 7 was about 13%.
  • Four whale claims (16.46 million XRP bought, a 140.35 million XRP transfer, 418.5 million XRP leaving Binance and a 2026 high in million-XRP wallets) could not be checked against a ledger record or open data. We mark them unverified. That does not mean false. Even if true, 16.46 million XRP is worth about $19 million, roughly 1% of a single day's XRP trading on CoinGecko.

Why we did this check

XRP lost about 4% in one hour on Sunday afternoon, from about $1.15 at 4 p.m. ET to $1.11 by 5 p.m. ET on November 8, 2026, according to CoinGecko. It kept sliding overnight to about $1.10. As it fell, posts and headlines said big holders were quietly buying.

We split those posts into separate claims and checked each one against CoinGecko price data. For the whale claims we looked for a ledger record we could open ourselves. We did not use news articles as evidence.

What the price record shows

The direction in the viral posts is right. XRP is down 12.3% in seven days, 21.8% in 30 days and 51.3% in a year on CoinGecko. It is now the sixth-largest coin, with a market value of about $69.8 billion.

The exact numbers are a little off. The $1.34 starting point does not appear in CoinGecko's hourly data for the week, which peaks at $1.32. Small gaps like this matter when a post uses them to make a move sound bigger.

Why the whale claims stay unverified

Every XRP transfer is public on the XRP Ledger, so a whale claim can in principle be checked by anyone with the transaction ID. None of the posts gave one, and we could not reach a ledger explorer from our system during this check. Until we can open the records, we neither confirm nor reject them.

Size matters too. The 16.46 million XRP figure sounds large, but CoinGecko counted about $1.8 billion of XRP trading in the last 24 hours. Buying worth about $19 million over five days is a small share of that.

LC Insight analysis

This section is our reading of the facts above, not a statement of fact.

  • We think the price claims are mostly fair and the whale claims are being asked to carry too much. "Whales are buying" posts often appear after a drop, but without transaction IDs readers cannot tell an exchange moving coins between its own wallets from a real buyer. This is not investment advice.
  • The 16.46 million XRP figure is the claim most often repeated, and at about $19 million it is roughly 1% of one day's XRP trading. Even if real, it is small next to the market.
  • Every XRP transfer is public, so whale posts that come without transaction IDs are easy to test and should be. Until someone shares the IDs, we keep these four marked unverified.

What's next

  • Whether XRP holds near $1.10 or revisits its August low near $0.99
  • Transaction IDs for the 140.35 million XRP transfer, so it can be checked on the ledger
  • Any statement from Binance about XRP wallet changes
