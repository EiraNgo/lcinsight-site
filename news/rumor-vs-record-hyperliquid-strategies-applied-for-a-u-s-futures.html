Rumor vs record: Hyperliquid Strategies applied for a U.S. futures license. It has not got one

Posts say the Nasdaq-listed HYPE holder got a U.S. futures license after a $620.6 million quarter. We checked each line against its own SEC filing. It applied. It has not been approved.

LC Insight Editorial Team3 min read

Key points

  • The filing says a subsidiary applied to become a futures commission merchant. It states there is no assurance of timing or outcome.
  • Net income of $620.6 million is real, but $803.0 million came from unrealized HYPE gains. Staking and validator income was $12.3 million.
  • HYPE was $90.99 on September 30 and about $68.6 on November 17 (CoinGecko), roughly 25% lower.

Why we did this check

Posts about Hyperliquid Strategies (Nasdaq: PURR) have mixed two facts into one headline: a record quarterly profit and a U.S. futures license. We could not measure how widely the claims spread, so we checked the wording as it circulates. We quote the claims, not the people who posted them.

The distinction matters because a futures commission merchant (FCM) must register with U.S. regulators before it can accept customer orders and hold customer funds. An application is a request to start that process. Nothing in the company's own filing says the request has been granted, or when a decision will arrive.

We read the release itself rather than any summary of it, and we note that its cumulative treasury table is labeled as of October 28, while a footnote refers to a different date for capital raising. That small inconsistency is in the company's own document, so we use only the figures that are stated without ambiguity.

What the record shows

The company's results release, filed with the SEC, reports net income of $620.6 million for the quarter ended September 30, 2026. The largest line is $803.0 million of unrealized gains on HYPE. Staking revenue and validator commissions were $12.3 million, and the income tax provision was $172.3 million.

On licensing, a subsidiary filed an application to become a futures commission merchant, a firm registered to hold customer money for futures trading. If approved, it would carry U.S. client accounts for permissioned markets on Hyperliquid under HIP-3. The company says there is no assurance on timing or outcome.

One more check: the release says the company cites about $834 million of value accruing to the Hyperliquid ecosystem over twelve months, and it states that figure rests on public information it has not independently verified. We therefore do not repeat it as fact.

For readers: a company profit headline, a license headline and a price headline are three separate claims. Each needs its own source.

The price since quarter end

The release values 38.52 million HYPE at $90.99 each, or $3,505.3 million. CoinGecko showed HYPE near $68.6 on November 17. By our arithmetic, that is roughly $860 million below the September 30 value of the quarter-end holdings. Paper gains move both ways.

LC Insight analysis

This section is our reading of the facts above, not a statement of fact.

  • We think the claim that it "got a license" comes from compressing two sentences into one. An application is the start of a review, not the end.
  • The profit headline is accurate but incomplete. It rests mostly on the HYPE price on one date, so readers should look at the gain type before the total.

What's next

  • Any regulator or company notice that approves, delays or rejects the application.
  • The next quarterly filing, which will show how the HYPE price drop changes the result.
