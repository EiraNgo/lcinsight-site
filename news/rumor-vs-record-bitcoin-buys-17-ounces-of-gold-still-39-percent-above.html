Rumor vs record: bitcoin buys 17 ounces of gold, still 39% above its March low

Bitcoin has lost 15% against gold since November 1, and gold has held up better all year. But bitcoin was cheaper in gold in March, and gold itself is 36% below its record. We checked five claims.

LC Insight Editorial Team4 min read

Key points

  • At 6:38 a.m. ET on November 23, CoinGecko priced one bitcoin at 16.96 troy ounces of gold, or $61,403. That is 59% below bitcoin's December 17, 2024 peak of 41.01 ounces.
  • It is not a record low. CoinGecko's daily data put bitcoin at 12.22 ounces on March 2, 2026, so today's level is about 39% higher.
  • Gold is not soaring either: the PAX Gold token, backed by one ounce each, traded at $3,610.61, 35.7% below its January 28, 2026 record. Over 30 days it fell 9.7% while bitcoin fell 23.8%.

The short answer

One bitcoin bought 16.96 troy ounces of gold at 6:38 a.m. ET on Monday, November 23, 2026, per CoinGecko. That is down about 15% since November 1, but it is not a record low: on March 2, 2026, bitcoin bought only 12.22 ounces.

Bitcoin trades about 51% below its October 6, 2025 record of $126,080, according to CoinGecko. With prices that low, posts comparing bitcoin with gold are circulating again, along with claims that the bottom is in. We took five common versions and checked each against CoinGecko's own price data at that time.

The bitcoin-to-gold ratio is simple: the dollar price of one bitcoin divided by the dollar price of one troy ounce of gold. When it falls, bitcoin is losing ground to gold, even if both are falling in dollars.

Bitcoin in gold, peak to now

Bitcoin's best level against gold came on December 17, 2024, at 41.01 ounces, per CoinGecko. In the past 12 months the ratio peaked at 22.22 ounces on December 4, 2025, fell to 12.22 ounces on March 2, 2026, then climbed back to 20.0 ounces by November 1. Since then it has lost about 15%, to 16.96.

Gold fell too, just less

The "gold is soaring" version misses the dollar side. PAX Gold (PAXG), a token where each unit is backed by one troy ounce held by its issuer, reached $5,619.09 on January 28, 2026. At 6:38 a.m. ET it was $3,610.61, down 35.7% from that record and 11.0% over a year. We looked at PAXG's reserves and holders in an earlier story.

So the two moved the same way, with bitcoin moving harder. Over 30 days bitcoin lost 23.8% and PAXG lost 9.7%. That gap is what pushed the ratio from 20 ounces to about 17.

Is the bottom in?

CoinGecko's hourly data puts the past week's low at about $55,711 near 1 a.m. ET on Saturday, November 21. At 6:38 a.m. ET Monday bitcoin was $61,403, about 10% higher. Those are the facts. Whether $55,711 holds is a guess about the future, so we mark the claim Unverified rather than true or false.

How we checked

All figures come from CoinGecko: the bitcoin page (dollar price, gold price, records and 30-day and one-year changes), its daily bitcoin-in-gold series for the past 12 months, and the PAX Gold page. Daily readings are taken at midnight UTC (7 p.m. ET the evening before). Different data feeds can differ slightly on the gold price, so small gaps of a few tenths of an ounce are normal.

LC Insight analysis

This section is our reading of the facts above, not a statement of fact.

  • We think the cleanest reading is relative: gold has held value better than bitcoin this year, but both are below their records, and neither claim of a crash or a surge tells the full story alone.
  • The March low of 12.22 ounces is the line that matters for the "record low" claim. Bitcoin is well above it today, so posts calling this the worst ever are mixing up dollars and gold.
  • Calls that the bottom is in are opinions. A seven-day low is a fact; whether it holds is not yet known.

What's next

  • Whether the ratio falls back toward March's 12.22 ounces or recovers toward 20.
  • PAX Gold's price against its January 28 record of $5,619.09.
  • Bitcoin against the $55,711 seven-day low on CoinGecko.
