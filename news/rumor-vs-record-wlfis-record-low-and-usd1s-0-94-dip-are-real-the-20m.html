Rumor vs record: WLFI's record low and USD1's $0.94 dip are real; the $20M ether sale is unproven

We checked six viral claims about World Liberty Financial's night against CoinGecko data. Two are true, one is partly true, one is misleading and two, including the claim that a $20 million ether sale caused the crash, we could not verify.

LC Insight Editorial Team3 min read

Key points

  • WLFI hit a record low of $0.01668 at 12:46 a.m. ET and was at $0.0191 at 6:36 a.m. ET, down 53% in 24 hours on CoinGecko. Its 24-hour trading peaked at $523 million by then, close to its $600 million market value.
  • USD1, the project's dollar stablecoin, did drop to $0.9394 at 2:05 a.m. ET, but it was back above $0.99 within 45 minutes and at $0.998 by 6:36 a.m. ET.
  • The claim that a World Liberty wallet sold about $20 million of ether and caused the crash circulated without a wallet address we could check. We found no statement from the project.

The claims

World Liberty Financial's WLFI token lost half its price overnight, as we reported at 1:20 a.m. ET. By morning, posts about the night were everywhere. We checked the six most shared claims against CoinGecko data. The table above has each verdict.

What changed since the crash

WLFI has not made a new low since 12:46 a.m. ET. CoinGecko's five-minute chart shows it trading between about $0.0179 and $0.0211 since 1:15 a.m. ET. It was at $0.0191 at 6:36 a.m. ET, with a market value of about $600 million.

Trading kept climbing after the fall. WLFI's rolling 24-hour volume was about $246 million at 1 a.m. ET and reached $523 million at 4:25 a.m. ET, the high as of 6:36 a.m. ET, nearly as much as the whole token's market value.

USD1 wobbled an hour after WLFI's low

The stablecoin's dip came more than an hour after WLFI's low. CoinGecko data shows USD1 sliding from $0.996 at 1:20 a.m. ET to $0.9395 at 2:05 a.m. ET, then bouncing to $0.986 by 2:15 a.m. ET. WLFI bounced at the same moment, from $0.0183 to $0.0203. USD1 has kept shrinking all month, as we reported.

What we could not check

We could not tie the ether sale story to a wallet, and we have no exchange data on forced selling. We also found no explanation from World Liberty Financial by 6:40 a.m. ET. We will update this check if the project or on-chain records give a clear answer.

LC Insight analysis

This section is our reading of the facts above, not a statement of fact.

  • We think the price claims hold up and the cause claims do not, at least not yet. That is the usual pattern on a crash night: the numbers are easy to check, the stories about why are not.
  • USD1's dip to $0.94 was short, but a dollar token trading 6% under its peg for even a few minutes is worth watching.
  • Readers should ask for a wallet address before trusting any "team sold" claim. Without one, it cannot be checked.

What's next

  • Whether WLFI holds above its $0.01668 record low.
  • Whether USD1 stays near $1 and whether its supply keeps falling.
  • Any official statement from World Liberty Financial.
