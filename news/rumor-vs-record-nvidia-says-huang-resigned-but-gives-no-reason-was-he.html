Rumor vs record: Nvidia says Huang resigned but gives no reason. Was he ousted?

Nvidia announced its CEO's resignation on November 12, 2026, and crypto feeds filled with claims about why and what it did to prices. We checked six of them against Nvidia's own filing.

LC Insight Editorial Team3 min read

Key points

  • Nvidia's board "accepted the resignation" of Jensen Huang as president, CEO and director, effective immediately, its 8-K filing says. It gives no reason.
  • Colette Kress, CFO since 2013, is interim CEO and stays CFO. No permanent successor has been named; the board is searching inside and outside the company.
  • Harvey C. Jones, a director since 1993, became Nvidia's first-ever board chair. Nothing in the record ties Huang's exit to bitcoin's drop the same day.

Why we did this check

Huang's exit landed in crypto feeds within minutes on Thursday, November 12. Tokenized Nvidia shares and Nvidia perpetual futures trade around the clock on crypto venues, so traders reacted before most stock investors could. Posts and headlines mixed facts with guesses about why he left, who replaces him and what it did to bitcoin.

We used two primary documents only: Nvidia's CEO transition release and its Form 8-K, both filed with the SEC. We did not use news reports as evidence.

What the filing actually says

The 8-K reports an event dated November 11 and was signed November 12. It is filed under Item 5.02, the section companies use when a top officer or director leaves. The board accepted Huang's resignation from all three roles at once. The filing does not state a reason and does not say whether he disagreed with the company. It discloses no severance or pay terms.

Kress, 59, becomes interim CEO and keeps the CFO job. Jones, 73, managing partner of Square Wave Ventures, is now chair. Jones said in the release that "the Board has full confidence in Colette and the leadership team."

Nvidia also kept its date: third-quarter results for fiscal 2027 come on Tuesday, November 17, with a call at 5 p.m. ET.

The background readers asked about

On Sunday, November 8, Huang posted a memo to staff admitting he had been unfaithful, and the board said it had opened an independent review. We covered that memo and the tokenized Nvidia drop that followed in our November 8 story. Days later he was gone. The timing invites a link, but the company has not drawn one in writing.

What we could not check

Some numbers spread widely on Thursday that we could not confirm on a primary source. Claims that Nvidia shares opened about 12% lower, and that Nvidia perpetual futures on one crypto venue traded billions of dollars in a day, come from market data we could not open when we checked. We are not calling them wrong. We are leaving them out of the verdicts until we can see the raw data.

The same goes for any claim about what Huang will do next. Neither document says anything about his plans, his shares or any advisory role.

Why this matters for crypto traders

Weekend and overnight markets in tokenized stocks react to headlines first and filings second. On Thursday the filing was out the same day, and it was short. Anything beyond it, such as motives, a named successor or a market cause, was not in the record when we checked.

LC Insight analysis

This section is our reading of the facts above, not a statement of fact.

  • We think the gap between the short filing and the long list of claims is the story. Nvidia said what changed, not why. Traders pricing tokenized Nvidia overnight were betting on reasons the company has not confirmed.
  • The likely next test is November 17. If Nvidia's results call addresses the change, that will be the first on-record word beyond the filing.

What's next

  • Nvidia's results and call on November 17
  • Any amended 8-K or board statement on the review
  • Tokenized Nvidia prices around the results
