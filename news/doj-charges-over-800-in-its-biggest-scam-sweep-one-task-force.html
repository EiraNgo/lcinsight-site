DOJ charges over 800 in its biggest scam sweep; one task force restrained $1.08 billion in crypto

The Justice Department said Tuesday it has charged more than 800 defendants in over 400 scam cases since March. One task force alone has traced and restrained $1,082,260,156.82 in crypto taken from U.S. victims and pulled down 700 fake crypto investment sites.

LC Insight Editorial Team4 min read

Key points

  • The Justice Department announced its "largest-ever crackdown" on scammers on Tuesday, November 24: over 800 defendants, over 400 cases, 85 federal districts, since March 6, 2026.
  • The U.S. Attorney's Office in Washington, D.C., said its Scam Center Strike Force has traced and restrained $1.08 billion in crypto stolen from U.S. victims, and took down 700 fake crypto investment websites in six months.
  • State Department-backed work helped freeze over $1 billion in stolen crypto to date. Prosecutors restrained $52 million of laundered crypto in a single day.

What the Justice Department announced

On Tuesday, November 24, Attorney General Todd Blanche announced what the Justice Department called its largest-ever crackdown on scammers. The release counts over 800 defendants in more than 400 cases across 85 federal districts. The work ran from March 6, 2026, when Executive Order 14390 was signed, to the date of the announcement. For crypto, the headline number came from one task force, which has traced and restrained $1.08 billion stolen from U.S. victims. New to crypto scams? Read how exit scams work.

"Predatory scams against the American people will not be tolerated," Blanche said. The release cites a Gallup estimate that Americans lost about $68 billion to scams in 2025, and that 10% of U.S. adults were scammed last year. Thirty defendants were extradited.

The crypto numbers

Crypto investment fraud is a large part of the push. The U.S. Attorney's Office in Washington said its Scam Center Strike Force has tracked down and frozen in place $1,082,260,156.82 in crypto that was taken from victims in the United States. Restrained means the funds are held so they cannot be moved while the case goes on.

The same office said the Strike Force:

  • seized and removed 700 fake crypto investment websites in the past six months;
  • seized Telegram channels of the Xinbi Guarantee Network, which it described as a market selling websites and laundering services to scam centers, and restrained $52 million in laundered crypto in one day;
  • helped take down 13 scam compounds in Madagascar;
  • charged six people with wire fraud over crypto confidence scams operated out of an African compound;
  • restrained more than $3 million in crypto during a disruption week with Meta, Google, Microsoft and Coinbase.

The main release adds that State Department-supported work with U.S. and Southeast Asian police has helped freeze over $1 billion in stolen crypto to date. The Secret Service restrained over $200 million in illicit funds during the surge, without saying how much was crypto.

Kiosks, extraditions and fake platforms

The Criminal Division's list of cases shows how the money moves. In a Georgia prison jury-duty scheme, victims were told to pay supposed fines through crypto kiosks, and inmates were indicted in November. One man, extradited from the Netherlands on October 22, faces money laundering charges tied to crypto investment fraud; prosecutors allege that he and others received more than $36 million in fraud money into a business account he helped open. In May, shared government data let private firms freeze more than $3.8 million in crypto.

The SEC also took part. Five days earlier, on November 19, it charged eight Colorado-based entities that it says ran fake crypto trading platforms and took more than $37 million from U.S. investors, using fake SEC certificates to look real. All of these are allegations; the defendants have not been found liable.

LC Insight analysis

This section is our reading of the facts above, not a statement of fact.

  • We think the $1.08 billion figure matters more than the defendant count. Most victims of crypto investment scams never see their money again, and restrained funds are the pool any repayment would come from.
  • The likely result is more pressure on stablecoin issuers and exchanges to freeze fast, since the release credits private partners, including Coinbase, with part of the holds.
  • Taking down 700 fake investment sites in six months shows the scale of the storefront side. The Xinbi seizure goes after a market that, prosecutors say, sold those sites and laundering to scam centers, which is the supply behind them.

What's next

  • Forfeiture filings that turn restrained crypto into money returned to victims
  • More Telegram and website seizures tied to scam-center laundering
  • The SEC's Colorado case as it moves through court
