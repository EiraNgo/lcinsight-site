HYPE fell 13% to $57, and its Hyperliquid futures bets shrank $76 million since Friday

Hyperliquid's own token fell harder than bitcoin and ether. Open bets on its main futures market dropped to $527 million, while top traders added to their shorts and now sit on paper gains as a group.

LC Insight Editorial Team3 min read

Key points

  • HYPE was at $56.79 early Saturday (12:41 a.m. ET), down 13.0% in 24 hours and 15.8% in a week, CoinGecko shows. Bitcoin fell 9.2% and ether 9.6% over the same day.
  • Open bets on Hyperliquid's HYPE-USD contract fell to $527 million from $603 million on Friday morning, CoinGecko's data show. Funding stayed slightly positive.
  • DeepSea's HYPE-PERP page counts 69 top traders 92% short with $231.77 million. Their average entry, $65.82, is about 16% above the price.

What moved

HYPE is the token of Hyperliquid, an exchange for perpetual futures, price bets that never expire. It fell to a 24-hour low of $55.88 and stood at $56.79 early Saturday, at 12:41 a.m. ET, CoinGecko shows. That is about 42% below its September 23 record of $97.96.

HYPE's 13.0% daily drop was steeper than bitcoin's 9.2% and ether's 9.6%.

Open bets shrank

At 12:41 a.m. ET, CoinGecko's listing for Hyperliquid showed open interest, the money still in bets not yet closed, on the HYPE-USD contract at $527 million. On Friday at 6:31 a.m. ET it was $603 million, and at 12:58 a.m. on Friday $613 million. The contract traded $866 million in 24 hours.

Part of the drop is the price. The same number of tokens is worth fewer dollars after a 13% fall. Friday morning's bets, valued at Saturday's price, would be worth about $530 million. So the price move explains almost all of the $76 million, and only a few million dollars of bets were closed outright.

HYPE's funding rate, the regular payment that pulls a perpetual back toward the spot price, stayed just above zero, so longs were still paying shorts. On bitcoin and ether it was slightly negative.

Top traders added shorts

DeepSea's HYPE-PERP page, read at 12:40 a.m. ET, counted 69 tracked wallets, 92% short with $231.77 million at an average entry of $65.82, against $19.71 million long at $68.39. On Friday at 12:39 a.m. it showed 61 wallets with $246.63 million short at $65.41.

Revalued at the current price, Friday's short book would come to about $215 million, so the short side grew by roughly $16 million in new or bigger bets. The page itself put open HYPE futures at $1.05 billion, down from $1.22 billion.

The biggest short is still the one entered at $28.79, now ranked Smart trader #5: $24.72 million at 10x, $12.25 million down on paper, about $3.4 million less than on Friday. The largest long, Smart trader #292, holds $6.45 million from $70.58 and shows a $1.55 million paper loss.

What we could not check

We tried to add the USDC balance of the Hyperliquid bridge on Arbitrum, following our November 18 story on $515 million leaving it. Arbiscan, DefiLlama and Hyperliquid's own data feed were out of reach for us this morning, so this story has no new bridge figure.

LC Insight analysis

This section is our reading of the facts above, not a statement of fact.

  • We think the open interest drop says more about the price than about traders leaving. Almost all of it is the same bets valued at a lower price.
  • Top traders leaned further into the fall. With their average short entry about 16% above the price, the group now has room to hold.
  • Positive funding on HYPE, while bitcoin and ether turned negative, suggests the wider HYPE market still leans long even as the tracked traders lean short.

What's next

  • Whether HYPE breaks Saturday's 24-hour low of $55.88.
  • Whether open bets on the HYPE-USD contract fall below $500 million.
  • Whether the Hyperliquid bridge balance on Arbitrum keeps shrinking once the data can be read again.
