One leaked API key cost a Lighter trader about $10.4 million. Lighter froze all withdrawals

Lighter says a single trader's leaked API key, not a bug in its contracts, let an attacker pull about $10.4 million in USDC on November 16, 2026. On-chain trackers show the money swapped to ether and sent to Hyperliquid.

LC Insight Editorial Team3 min read

Key points

  • Lighter, an order-book perpetual futures exchange, paused all withdrawals and pool creation at about 6:02 a.m. ET on November 16, 2026, while it looked into a security incident.
  • At 7:49 a.m. ET it said a leaked API key for one trader's account caused the incident, that its protocol and smart contracts were unaffected, and that it had switched on Guardian mode.
  • DefiLlama lists the loss at about $10.39 million. Bubblemaps tracked about $10.4 million in USDC swapped to ether and sent in batches to Hyperliquid, where it was turned into a token called XMR1.

What Lighter said

At about 6:02 a.m. ET on Monday, November 16, 2026, Lighter posted on X that it was investigating a security incident that seemed to involve a single compromised external party. As a precaution, it suspended all withdrawals and the creation of new pools.

Less than two hours later, at 7:49 a.m. ET, the team gave an update. It said the cause was a leaked API key for a specific trader's account, and that the Lighter protocol and smart contracts were unaffected. It also said it had enabled Guardian mode on its smart contract, a safety switch, and that recovering funds was its main priority.

An API key is a password that lets a trading bot place orders for an account. Whoever holds it can trade as that account without logging in. Lighter has not said how the key leaked or whose account it was, and we do not name the trader.

Where the money went

The open record of the theft comes from on-chain trackers. DefiLlama's hacks list files the incident under Lighter Perps on November 16 at about $10.39 million, classed as a frontend and infrastructure attack with the technique "API key compromised".

Bubblemaps' exploiter report, posted at 7:00 a.m. ET, says $10.4 million in USDC was withdrawn from Lighter's main wallet. It says the dollars were swapped to ether (ETH) and moved in batches to Hyperliquid, another futures exchange, where they were converted to XMR1, a token named after the privacy coin Monero. The first batch it lists is 4,484 ETH, worth about $8.12 million at the time. Bitquery's investigation also puts the withdrawal at $10.4 million.

Privacy coins hide who sends what to whom, which is why a jump into a Monero-themed token is a red flag for anyone trying to trace or freeze stolen funds. Nobody has publicly named the attacker.

What LIT did

Lighter's own token, LIT, swung hard. CoinGecko shows a 24-hour range of $2.35 to $3.05. At 7:15 p.m. ET it traded at $2.98, up 5.6% over 24 hours, so the price had already climbed back above where it stood a day earlier.

LC Insight analysis

This section is our reading of the facts above, not a statement of fact.

  • We think the lesson is about keys, not code. Lighter's contracts held, but one stolen trading key was enough to pull about $10 million out.
  • Pausing every withdrawal to stop one account is a heavy tool. It protects the rest of the exchange but locks out every user until Lighter is sure the leak is closed.
  • The fast route through Hyperliquid into a Monero-themed token shows how little time exchanges have to freeze funds once a key is in the wrong hands.

What's next

  • A post from Lighter confirming that withdrawals have reopened (we could not confirm this from Lighter's own posts at publication)
  • Any recovery or freeze of the funds, and whether Hyperliquid acts on the XMR1 trades
  • Whether Lighter changes its API key rules, such as withdrawal limits for keys
