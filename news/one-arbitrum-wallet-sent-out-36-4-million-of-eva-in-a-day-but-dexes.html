One Arbitrum wallet sent out $36.4 million of EVA in a day, but DEXes traded just $80,000

DeepSea's 24-hour feed shows one unlabeled wallet sending out about 1 million EverValueCoin in 11 moves. The token's DEX pools could never have absorbed that as a sale.

LC Insight Editorial Team3 min read

Key points

  • One unlabeled Arbitrum wallet moved out $36,416,886 of EVA (EverValueCoin) in 11 transactions in the 24 hours to 12:41 a.m. ET on November 15, 2026, and took in none, DeepSea's flow table shows.
  • At DeepSea's price of $36.38, that is roughly 1 million EVA, about 5% of the 21 million maximum supply CoinGecko lists.
  • CoinGecko counts only $80,379 of EVA trading in 24 hours, all on Arbitrum DEXes, and DeepSea puts pool liquidity at $4.81 million, so the tokens were not dumped on those markets.

What DeepSea's data shows

DeepSea's 24-hour flow table puts EVA at the top of Arbitrum's sell side: $36,416,886 out, $0 in, all from one wallet in 11 transactions. DeepSea tags it as a shark, but its wallet page says the address has no label in its public dataset.

On DeepSea's EVA token page, the coin trades at $36.38, down 2.6% in 24 hours, with a market cap of $562.98 million and a fully diluted value of $651.25 million. Liquidity is just $4.81 million and 24-hour volume $73.8K. Its 7-day flow box shows whales at minus $18.26 million net, with smart money and KOLs at $0.

Data: DeepSea (desea.io), checked November 15, 2026, 12:41 a.m. ET.

What moved

One unlabeled Arbitrum wallet sent out $36.4 million of EVA (EverValueCoin) in 11 transactions in the 24 hours to 12:41 a.m. ET on November 15, 2026, and took in none, DeepSea's flow table shows. EVA's DEX pools traded only about $80,000 that day, so the tokens were not dumped on those markets.

EVA is a token on Arbitrum. Its CoinGecko listing describes a hard cap of 21 million tokens and a burn vault backed by wrapped bitcoin (WBTC), where holders can burn EVA for a share of the reserve.

That outflow is worth about 7.5 times all the liquidity in EVA's pools. At DeepSea's price of $36.38 a token, it is about 1 million EVA. CoinGecko puts total supply at about 17.9 million, so the move equals roughly 5.6% of all EVA in existence.

The numbers

A sale that size would crush a thin market. It did not happen on the DEXes that list EVA. CoinGecko showed EVA at $36.07, down 3.9% in 24 hours, at 12:59 a.m. ET, with $80,379 of trading in that day. Every market it lists is an Arbitrum DEX pool, mostly on Uniswap.

So the $36.4 million outflow is about 450 times the day's DEX volume. The price barely moved.

What we do not know

The data we could open do not show where the roughly 1 million EVA went. DeepSea logs the move as selling, but no DEX shows matching volume. A transfer to another wallet, a contract or a burn would not show up as DEX trading. We could not confirm the destination, and nobody has said publicly who controls the wallet.

Why it matters

EVA's market cap of more than half a billion dollars rests on pools holding under $5 million. When a single wallet controls tokens worth seven times that liquidity, its next move matters more than any chart. Holders can watch the same wallet page on DeepSea for the next transfer.

LC Insight analysis

This section is our reading of the facts above, not a statement of fact.

  • We think the gap between a $36.4 million outflow and about $80,000 of DEX volume points to a transfer, not a market sale. A sale of even a tenth of that size would have moved the price far more than 3%.
  • A token valued at over $560 million with under $5 million of liquidity is fragile. Market cap here is a price times supply, not money that could be taken out.
  • The 7-day whale outflow of $18.26 million on DeepSea's token page suggests large holders have been moving EVA all week, not only today.

What's next

  • Where the 1 million EVA lands next
  • Whether EVA's pool liquidity stays near $5 million
  • Any burn through the token's burn vault that cuts supply
