Bitcoin ETFs lost $157.5 million Wednesday, pushing November's outflow to $2.55 billion

U.S. spot bitcoin funds had a second straight outflow day as bitcoin closed near $62,500. Ether funds lost $17 million, their third outflow day in a row.

LC Insight Editorial Team3 min read

Key points

  • U.S. spot bitcoin ETFs had a net outflow of $157.5 million on Wednesday, November 18, 2026, CoinMarketCap data show. Tuesday's revised figure, $241.9 million out, held.
  • U.S. spot ether ETFs lost $17.0 million, a third straight outflow day.
  • In November, bitcoin funds have lost $2.55 billion in 13 sessions and ether funds $684.1 million. Each group had only two inflow days.

Wednesday's numbers

U.S. spot bitcoin ETFs had a net outflow of $157.5 million on Wednesday, November 18, 2026, CoinMarketCap's ETF flow data showed at 12:36 a.m. ET on Thursday. Spot ether funds lost $17.0 million, by the same source's ether series.

Each of these funds holds actual coins and trades on U.S. stock exchanges. An outflow day is one where investors pulled out more cash than they put in. For the basics, see what a bitcoin ETF is.

Tuesday's figure for bitcoin funds stayed at minus $241.9 million, the revised number we reported on Wednesday morning. Wednesday's totals are first readings and can still change as late issuers report.

Streaks and November totals

For bitcoin funds, Wednesday was a second outflow day in a row, after Monday's $9.6 million inflow. The two days together took out $399.4 million. November now stands at minus $2.55 billion over 13 sessions, with inflows only on November 4 ($75.2 million) and November 16.

Ether funds have now lost money three sessions in a row: $8.4 million on Monday, $26.3 million on Tuesday and $17.0 million on Wednesday. Their November total is minus $684.1 million, and its only inflow days were November 4 and 13.

The price on the day

Bitcoin fell from about $64,359 at midnight ET on Wednesday to about $62,503 at the 4 p.m. ET market close, a drop of about 2.9%, according to hourly prices on CoinGecko. Ether went from about $1,802 to about $1,751 over the same hours, about 2.8% lower.

Both bounced overnight. At 12:35 a.m. ET on Thursday, CoinGecko showed bitcoin at $63,364 (down 2.1% in 24 hours) and ether at $1,798 (down 1.0%).

The biggest funds, and what we could not see

BlackRock's IBIT is still the biggest bitcoin fund, holding $50.9 billion according to CoinMarketCap's fund list, ahead of Fidelity's FBTC at $14.9 billion. All U.S. spot bitcoin funds on the list hold about $87.2 billion. Among ether funds, BlackRock's ETHA leads with $8.6 billion of about $14.5 billion in total.

The CoinMarketCap series is one daily sum across all funds. Farside's fund-by-fund tables could not be opened from our system this morning, so we cannot say which fund drove Wednesday's outflow, or which issuers had not yet reported.

LC Insight analysis

This section is our reading of the facts above, not a statement of fact.

  • We think the pattern is steady selling. It does not look like panic. Wednesday's outflow was smaller than Tuesday's even as bitcoin kept falling, which suggests holders are trimming rather than rushing out.
  • At minus $2.55 billion in 13 sessions, November is on course to be a heavy month for bitcoin funds unless inflows return soon.
  • Ether funds lose less each day, but three outflow days in a row and only two inflow days all month show little new demand.

What's next

  • Whether Wednesday's first reading is revised, as Tuesday's was.
  • Fund-by-fund numbers, to see whether IBIT or another fund led the outflow.
  • Whether ether funds' losses for November top $700 million.
