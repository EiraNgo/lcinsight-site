A $216 million bitcoin whale shows a $291,600 weekly loss, but its moves look like loans

DeepSea counts an 82% win rate and a 7-day loss for an Ethereum wallet holding only cbBTC. Its trade list reads like a borrower moving collateral in and out of a lending market.

LC Insight Editorial Team3 min read

Key points

  • DeepSea's shark wallet Hold5f60 on Ethereum holds $215.96 million, all in cbBTC (Coinbase's wrapped bitcoin), and shows a 7-day loss of $291,600 despite an 82% win rate.
  • In six days it took out 600 cbBTC ($46.61 million) while closing out about 30 million USDS of debt, then put back 120 cbBTC ($9.40 million) and 220.46 cbBTC ($16.77 million) while new USDS debt of about $13 million appeared.
  • DeepSea also logs transfers of a token named to look like USDC, in amounts that mirror the wallet's real moves, with no dollar value.

What DeepSea's data shows

DeepSea labels the wallet Hold5f60, a shark on Ethereum with the address ending in 5f60. Its wallet page, read at about 6:40 a.m. ET on November 8, 2026, lists total assets of $215.96 million, all of it in cbBTC. At CoinGecko's price of $75,907, that is about 2,845 cbBTC.

DeepSea's trade stats: minus $291,600 over 7 days, minus $234,700 over 30 days, an 82% win rate, 116 trades and $288.48 million of volume in 30 days. Its realized PnL table (profit or loss on closed trades) (6:31 a.m. ET) counts 8 closed trades in 7 days, 6 of them winners.

The trade list explains the odd mix. It shows cbBTC swapped one for one with spcbBTC, and USDS paired with variableDebtUSDS. Those are the names of a deposit receipt and a loan record in the Spark lending market (Zerion lists variableDebtUSDS as "Spark Variable Debt USDS").

Data: DeepSea (desea.io), checked November 8, 2026, 6:40 a.m. ET.

Six days of moves

Reading DeepSea's list from oldest to newest (ages as shown at 6:40 a.m. ET):

  • 6 days ago: 600 spcbBTC out, 600 cbBTC in ($46.61 million); 30 million USDS in and out, and 29.98 million variableDebtUSDS gone. That reads like repaying a loan of about $30 million and taking the bitcoin collateral back. It also cashed out $5 million of aEthUSDC, the receipt for USDC deposited in Aave.
  • 4 days ago: 120 cbBTC ($9.40 million) swapped for 120 spcbBTC, with 5.01 million and 2 million variableDebtUSDS added. Another $5 million of aEthUSDC was cashed out, and $2 million more three days ago.
  • 2 days ago: 220.46 cbBTC ($16.77 million) swapped for spcbBTC, 6.01 million variableDebtUSDS added and $3.01 million of aEthUSDC cashed out.

Where the loss likely comes from

DeepSea logs the cbBTC as bought at about $77,683 each six days ago ($46.61 million for 600) and as sold at about $78,333 (four days ago) and $76,068 (two days ago) when it went into spcbBTC. Counted as sales, the second batch would book a loss of roughly $355,000 and the first a gain of about $78,000, close to the $291,600 DeepSea shows. But the wallet still holds only cbBTC, and the deposits appear to be collateral, not sales.

Lookalike USDC entries

The list also shows "sells" of a token whose name looks like USDC but is spelled with lookalike and hidden characters: 7 million and 6 million eight days ago, 5 million, 2 million and 5 million three to four days ago, and 3.01 million twice plus 6 million a day ago. DeepSea gives none of them a dollar value. Several amounts match the wallet's real USDC and USDS moves. Copying amounts like this is a known trick in address poisoning scams.

LC Insight analysis

This section is our reading of the facts above, not a statement of fact.

  • We think the 7-day loss is mostly an accounting effect: moving bitcoin into a lending market can look like a sale to a tracker that sees one token go out and another come in.
  • The bigger signal is leverage. The wallet repaid about $30 million of USDS early in the week, then borrowed roughly $13 million again against fresh collateral as bitcoin fell. It is borrowing less than before, but still borrowing.
  • The lookalike USDC entries match a known poisoning pattern. We cannot tell from DeepSea's data whether the wallet ever sent money to a fake address, and nothing shows that it did.

What's next

  • Whether the wallet adds more cbBTC to Spark or repays the new USDS debt
  • cbBTC's price: borrowers against bitcoin face liquidation if it falls far enough
  • Any real transfer to an address that mimics this wallet's usual counterparties
