Rumor vs record: MSCI's cut makes funds sell Strategy shares, not Strategy's bitcoin

After MSCI dropped Strategy from its USA index, posts warned of $2.8 billion to $8.8 billion in "forced selling" and asked what it means for bitcoin. We checked four claims against MSCI's own documents and Strategy's latest SEC filing.

LC Insight Editorial Team3 min read

Key points

  • "Strategy A" is one of 24 stocks leaving the MSCI USA Index when markets close on November 30, 2026. Index funds sell the stock, not bitcoin.
  • Strategy's November 9 filing records 1,036 bitcoin bought in the week to November 8 and no bitcoin sold. It holds 850,364 BTC.
  • Neither MSCI's documents nor Strategy's filing gives the $2.8 billion or $8.8 billion selling figures being shared.

Why we did this check

MSCI published its November review on Wednesday, November 11, 2026. Within hours, posts were warning of billions in "forced selling" and asking whether Strategy, the biggest corporate holder of bitcoin, would now have to dump its coins. Some put a number on it: $2.8 billion, or as much as $8.8 billion. We checked each claim against MSCI's own documents and Strategy's latest filing with the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC). For the basic facts of the index change, see our earlier report.

What MSCI actually published

MSCI's list of changes shows "STRATEGY A" as one of 24 stocks leaving the MSCI USA Index, against one joining. The new lineup applies "as of the close of business on November 30, 2026". The review announcement came out the same day.

At 5:23 p.m. ET on November 11, MSCI also announced its final list of securities affected by its new rule for non-operating companies. The rule first asks whether a company has substantial operating assets. Those that do not face five financial ratio tests, and four flags make a stock ineligible. An existing index member drops out only after failing that screen for two annual filing periods in a row. The announcement does not name companies and gives no estimate of fund selling.

Shares, not coins

A fund that copies an index must mirror its members. So when a stock exits, tracking funds sell it, typically near the date the change takes effect. Here that means shares of Strategy. Nothing in MSCI's documents requires Strategy to sell bitcoin.

Strategy's own record points the other way. Its Form 8-K filed November 9 shows 1,036 BTC added in the week to November 8 at $79,012 apiece, $81.9 million in all, lifting its total to 850,364 BTC bought for $64.16 billion. In the same week it sold 2,051,348 of its own class A shares for $283.8 million in net proceeds. The filing reports no bitcoin sales and does not mention MSCI.

Where the coins stand

CoinGecko showed bitcoin at $72,896 at 6:45 a.m. ET on November 12. At that price Strategy's 850,364 BTC are worth about $61.99 billion, roughly $2.17 billion less than it paid. A paper loss does not by itself force a sale, and no filing we read says the company plans one.

LC Insight analysis

This section is our reading of the facts above, not a statement of fact.

  • We think the confusion comes from mixing up two kinds of selling: index funds selling a stock, and a company selling its bitcoin. The record so far shows only the first is scheduled.
  • Selling of Strategy's shares could still matter for bitcoin indirectly, because the company raises money for its purchases by selling new stock. In our view, a weaker share price would make that funding harder, not trigger a bitcoin sale.
  • The $2.8 billion and $8.8 billion figures may prove close, but they are estimates from outside the two companies. Readers should treat them as forecasts, not facts.

What's next

  • Strategy's next weekly 8-K, usually filed on Mondays, for buys, sales or any comment on MSCI.
  • MSCI's small cap and other index lists, for any other bitcoin-holding companies.
  • Trading in Strategy shares around the November 30 close.
