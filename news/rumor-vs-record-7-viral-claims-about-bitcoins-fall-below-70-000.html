Rumor vs record: 7 viral claims about bitcoin's fall below $70,000, checked

Posts about Friday's crypto drop spread fast: a first since August, a 45% fall from the record, $1.2 billion liquidated, Nvidia to blame. We checked seven claims against price data and official records.

LC Insight Editorial Team4 min read

Key points

  • True: CoinGecko's hourly history shows bitcoin under $70,000 for the first time since August 20, and ether under $2,000 for the first time since August 19.
  • True: bitcoin at $69,145 is 45% below its $126,080 record from October 6, 2025, per CoinGecko.
  • Unverified: the "$1.2 billion liquidated" figure and a $31 million single liquidation come from a data aggregator we could not open, and no exchange has confirmed them.
  • No record ties Nvidia's CEO change to the drop. Inflation data and Fed hike talk are real, but they are context, not proof of cause.

What happened

Bitcoin fell under $70,000 late on Thursday, November 12, and reached $67,922 overnight, CoinGecko shows. Within hours, posts were sharing numbers and reasons. Some were right, some were rounded, and some could not be checked. We tested the most shared claims against price data and official records. Our full story on the drop is here.

The price claims hold up

The "first time since August" line is correct. In CoinGecko's hourly prices, bitcoin was last under $70,000 at 4 a.m. ET on August 20. Ether was last under $2,000 on August 19, and it fell as low as $1,905 on Friday, per CoinGecko.

The "45% from the record" line is also right. Bitcoin's record on CoinGecko is $126,080 from October 6, 2025. Some posts used $126,198. Exchanges and data sites record slightly different highs, so both are close, but CoinGecko's figure is the one we use.

The liquidation numbers are not confirmed

A liquidation is when an exchange closes a trader's borrowed bet because the money behind it has run out. The most shared posts put the 24-hour total at more than $1.2 billion and the largest single one at $31 million on Hyperliquid.

Those numbers trace back to a private data aggregator, not to an exchange's own report. We could not open the aggregator or Hyperliquid's public data during this check. That does not make the numbers wrong. It means we cannot confirm them, so we mark them unverified.

The causes are context, not proof

Nvidia's 8-K filing says its board accepted Jensen Huang's resignation as CEO, effective immediately, and its announcement names Colette Kress interim CEO. Neither gives a reason, and neither mentions crypto. Bitcoin fell below $70,000 more than a day after the filing's November 11 event date.

The rate story has more support. The Bureau of Labor Statistics said core consumer prices rose 0.3% in October. Hours before that release, on November 10, Fed Governor Christopher Waller said a half-point hike should be considered if core prices rose about 0.3% or more. Higher rates tend to weigh on risky assets. Still, markets move for many reasons at once, and no record can pin one night's drop on one cause.

LC Insight analysis

This section is our reading of the facts above, not a statement of fact.

  • We think the price claims spread fastest because they are easy to check, and this time they were right. The numbers worth doubting are the ones that cannot be traced to a primary record.
  • Liquidation totals are useful as a rough guide to how much leverage was in the market, but different trackers count differently. Treat any single figure as an estimate until an exchange publishes its own data.
  • Blaming one headline for a market move is tempting. The record supports a slower story: hotter inflation, hike talk and fund outflows building over a week.

What's next

  • Whether exchanges or Hyperliquid's public data confirm the size of the liquidations.
  • New Fed comments on a December hike.
  • Whether bitcoin holds above its August 19 levels.
