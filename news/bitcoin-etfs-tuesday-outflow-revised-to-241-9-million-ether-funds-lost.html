Bitcoin ETFs' Tuesday outflow revised to $241.9 million; ether funds lost $26.3 million

Revised CoinMarketCap data show U.S. spot bitcoin funds lost $241.9 million on Tuesday, November 17, not $32.4 million. Ether funds lost $26.3 million instead of taking in $1.3 million.

LC Insight Editorial Team3 min read

Key points

  • U.S. spot bitcoin ETFs had a net outflow of $241.9 million on Tuesday, November 17, 2026, CoinMarketCap's revised data show. The early figure was $32.4 million.
  • U.S. spot ether ETFs lost $26.3 million on Tuesday. The early figure was an inflow of $1.3 million.
  • In November, bitcoin funds have lost $2.40 billion and ether funds $667.1 million, in 12 sessions each.

The revised Tuesday numbers

U.S. spot bitcoin ETFs had a net outflow of $241.9 million on Tuesday, November 17, 2026, revised CoinMarketCap data show, about seven times the $32.4 million first reported. Ether funds lost $26.3 million after first showing a small inflow.

Spot bitcoin and ether ETFs trade like shares in New York, and each one owns real coins. A net outflow means more money left the funds than came in that day. New to these funds? Read what a bitcoin ETF is and how daily flows work.

At 12:35 a.m. ET on Wednesday, CoinMarketCap's ETF flow data showed a bitcoin fund outflow of $32.4 million for Tuesday, November 17. At 6:42 a.m. ET the same data showed $241.9 million. Daily totals change as issuers that report late add their numbers.

Ether funds moved the other way. The early reading was an inflow of $1.3 million. The revised ether figure is an outflow of $26.3 million.

Losing streaks and November totals

For bitcoin funds, Tuesday was the biggest outflow since November 11 ($308.6 million). It followed Monday's $9.6 million inflow, which had ended a seven-session outflow run. Only two of November's 12 sessions, November 4 and November 16, were inflow days. The month's net total is now minus $2.40 billion.

Ether funds have now had outflows on two sessions in a row, after a $2.3 million inflow on Friday, November 13. Their November total is minus $667.1 million, with inflows only on November 4 and 13.

The same data list Solana funds at zero net flow on Tuesday and XRP funds at minus $6.1 million.

The biggest funds and the price

BlackRock's IBIT is by far the largest bitcoin fund, with $50.9 billion in assets in CoinMarketCap's fund list, more than three times Fidelity's FBTC at $14.9 billion. Among ether funds, CoinMarketCap's list puts BlackRock's ETHA first with $8.6 billion, ahead of Grayscale's ETH at $2.2 billion.

Bitcoin slid from about $65,005 to about $63,912 between midnight ET Tuesday and the 4 p.m. ET market close, by CoinGecko's hourly prices. Ether went from about $1,834 to about $1,798 over the same hours. At 6:43 a.m. ET on Wednesday, CoinGecko showed bitcoin at $64,154 and ether at $1,798, both down about 0.9% in 24 hours.

What we could not confirm

The CoinMarketCap series is a single daily sum across every fund. Issuer flow pages and Farside's tables were out of reach for us this morning, so the fund behind Tuesday's outflow is still unknown. Tuesday's figures can still change.

LC Insight analysis

This section is our reading of the facts above, not a statement of fact.

  • We think the revision undoes Tuesday's calm reading. A $241.9 million outflow right after Monday's small inflow looks more like selling resuming than easing.
  • Two first readings in a row have now moved by more than $70 million. We treat early daily totals as provisional until late issuers report.
  • Ether funds are the quieter worry. At minus $667.1 million for November with only two inflow days, they are bleeding steadily, not in one big day.

What's next

  • Wednesday, November 18 flows, and whether Tuesday's total moves again.
  • Fund-by-fund numbers for Tuesday once issuer pages or Farside can be read.
  • Whether ether funds' November outflow passes $700 million.
