Bitcoin ETFs lost $451.6 million Wednesday, the seventh straight outflow day, $2.56 billion in all

The final count for Wednesday, November 25, is almost four times the early read. Ether funds lost $68.5 million, and U.S. markets are closed on Thursday for Thanksgiving, so the next flow figure comes Friday.

LC Insight Editorial Team3 min read

Key points

  • CoinMarketCap's final count shows a net $451.6 million leaving U.S. spot bitcoin ETFs on Wednesday, November 25.
  • That is the seventh outflow day in a row. Since November 17, $2.56 billion has left the funds.
  • Ether funds shed $68.5 million on Wednesday. That makes eight outflow days in a row, worth $403.4 million.
  • U.S. stock markets are closed on Thursday, November 26, for Thanksgiving, so there is no flow figure for the day.

Wednesday's final figure

U.S. spot bitcoin exchange-traded funds (ETFs) shed $451.6 million net on Wednesday, November 25. That is the final figure in CoinMarketCap's ETF flow data, checked at 6:43 a.m. ET on Thursday, November 26. A spot bitcoin ETF holds real bitcoin, and a net outflow means more shares were cashed in than bought.

The first read of Wednesday's number, published in our ETF & Whale Flows column overnight, was $121.9 million. The final total is almost four times larger, so we have corrected that column. Monday's number did the same: a first read of $60.4 million became $569.7 million.

Wednesday was the third-largest outflow of the run, after $631.9 million on November 19 and $569.7 million on November 23.

Seven days, $2.56 billion

Money has left bitcoin funds on every trading day since November 17. The seven days add up to $2.56 billion. November 16, with $9.6 million in, was the last day money came in.

Zoom out to CoinMarketCap's 30-day view, 21 trading days in all, and only four were inflow days. The other 17 were outflows, for a net $5.21 billion out.

Which fund led the selling

CoinMarketCap does not show the split by fund for Wednesday, and we could not open a fund-by-fund table on a primary source. The issuer's own numbers point to BlackRock's iShares Bitcoin Trust (IBIT), the largest fund.

IBIT's holdings file dated November 23 lists 1,344,480,000 shares outstanding. Its fund page lists 1,324,560,000 shares as of November 25, with a net asset value (NAV) of $33.93 per share. That is 19.92 million fewer shares, worth about $676 million at Wednesday's NAV.

All bitcoin funds together lost $686.1 million over Tuesday and Wednesday. So IBIT's shrinking share count covers nearly all of the two-day outflow. iShares does not break the change out by day.

Ether funds: day eight

Ether funds saw $68.5 million in net outflows on Wednesday, per CoinMarketCap's ether ETF data, after $38.3 million on Tuesday. Ether funds have now lost money eight trading days running, $403.4 million in all since November 16.

Across the 30-day view they gained on just two days and lost on 19, a net $1.28 billion out.

No flows on Thanksgiving

U.S. exchanges are closed on Thursday, November 26, for Thanksgiving Day, according to the NYSE holiday calendar. ETF shares do not trade, so there is no flow figure for Thursday. On Friday, November 27, stock markets close early, at 1:00 p.m. ET.

Bitcoin itself trades all day, every day. At 6:51 a.m. ET on Thursday, it was at $60,671 on CoinGecko, up 3.4% in 24 hours, with a 24-hour range of $57,308 to $61,375. It is still down 5.2% over seven days. Ether was at $1,699.15, up 3.5% in a day and down 6.2% in a week.

So bitcoin climbed back above $60,000 even as Wednesday's outflow was one of the run's largest.

LC Insight analysis

This section is our reading of the facts above, not a statement of fact.

  • We think the gap between the first and final Wednesday figures is the week's main lesson. Early ETF reads missed most of the selling twice, on Monday and Wednesday, so the final count tells the real story.
  • Bitcoin rose about 3% in a day while the funds kept shrinking. ETF flows show what one group of investors did, not the whole market.
  • With one large fund behind most of the recent outflows, one issuer's redemptions can set the tone for the whole group.

What's next

  • Friday's flow figures, the first after the holiday, on a shortened trading day.
  • Whether the bitcoin ETF outflow run reaches eight days.
  • IBIT's next shares-outstanding figure on the iShares fund page.
