Sharks bought $3.08 million of tokenized SpaceX on Arbitrum. Its DEX pool traded $56,000

Two shark wallets kept buying rSPCX and rCRCL, tokens CoinGecko lists as tokenized SpaceX and Circle stock, even as both fell. Almost none of it went through the public pool.

LC Insight Editorial Team3 min read

Key points

  • In the 24 hours to 6:34 a.m. ET on November 6, 2026, shark wallets bought $3.08 million of rSPCX on Arbitrum and sold $1.62 million, DeepSea's live feed shows. Net: about $1.45 million bought.
  • Two wallets also bought $1.06 million of rCRCL and sold $598,917, a net of about $461,000.
  • CoinGecko lists rSPCX as SpaceX Tokenized Stock (Reality), worth $18.32 million in all. Its Arbitrum Uniswap pool traded only $57,875 in 24 hours.

What DeepSea's data shows

DeepSea's live feed adds up trades by wallets it tags as sharks (large, active traders). At 6:34 a.m. ET on November 6, 2026, its 24-hour buying list for Arbitrum was topped by two tokens that stand for shares, not crypto projects.

rSPCX: $3,078,432 bought and $1,624,970 sold by 2 wallets in 11 trades. rCRCL: $1,059,653 bought and $598,917 sold by 2 wallets in 9 trades. Smaller amounts went into rHOOD ($49,303 bought, $38,751 sold), while rAAPL saw $22,727 of selling. DeepSea gives no names for the wallets.

This was not a one-day event. In DeepSea's reading a day earlier, at 6:36 a.m. ET on November 5, sharks had bought $1.51 million of rSPCX and sold $1.18 million. DeepSea's rSPCX token page showed a 7-day whale net flow of +$328,900.

Data: DeepSea (desea.io), checked November 6, 2026, 6:43 a.m. ET.

What are rSPCX and rCRCL?

CoinGecko lists the rSPCX contract on Arbitrum as SpaceX Tokenized Stock (Reality) and rCRCL as Circle Internet Group Tokenized Stock (Reality), both under its Tokenized Stocks category. Circle is the company behind the USDC dollar token. A tokenized stock is a crypto token meant to track the price of a company's shares.

LC Insight could not open the issuer's own site or a block explorer during this check, so we do not describe how the tokens are backed or who is allowed to hold them. Readers should check the issuer's terms before treating these tokens as shares. The figures in this piece come from DeepSea's live feed and token pages and from CoinGecko's price and market data.

Bought into a falling price

Both tokens were down when DeepSea logged the buying. At 6:44 a.m. ET, CoinGecko showed rSPCX at $129.77, down 3.4% in 24 hours, 6.1% in a week and 22.9% in 30 days. Its record is $229.25, set June 16, 2026. rCRCL was at $69.25, down 7.6% in a day, after an all-time low of $64.93 on November 5.

CoinGecko counts 141,217 rSPCX tokens, for a value of $18.32 million. At today's price, the $3.08 million the sharks bought would be roughly one-sixth of that supply, by our estimate.

Where did the trades happen?

Not in the public pools CoinGecko tracks. Its market list shows rSPCX's Uniswap V3 pool on Arbitrum traded just $57,875 in 24 hours, and DeepSea's own page put on-chain volume at $56,200 with $191,500 of liquidity. Most rSPCX trading CoinGecko tracks was on Bitget (RSPCX/USDT, $2.05 million). For rCRCL, the Arbitrum pool did $29,977 and Bitget $3.03 million.

So the $3.08 million DeepSea counted as shark buying is about 50 times the pool's daily volume. The data we read does not show how the tokens reached the wallets, or whether DeepSea counted transfers as buys.

LC Insight analysis

This section is our reading of the facts above, not a statement of fact.

  • We think the gap between the shark totals and the pool volume is the most useful part of this data. Big wallets are moving these tokens, but the public pools CoinGecko tracks show only a small part of it.
  • Steady net buying for two days while prices fell looks like accumulation, not a quick trade. The data does not show the owners or their reasons.
  • Tokenized stocks are a small market on Arbitrum. A few wallets can hold a large share, which matters for anyone trading the thin pools.

  • Any change in the tokens' Arbitrum pool liquidity
