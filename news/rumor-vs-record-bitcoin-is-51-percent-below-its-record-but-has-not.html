Rumor vs record: bitcoin is 51% below its record but has not broken $60,000

Bitcoin slid to about $61,500 overnight, and posts about the drop spread fast. We checked six of the most repeated claims against price and fund data: four are true, one is partly true and one is wrong.

LC Insight Editorial Team4 min read

Key points

  • Bitcoin traded at $61,568 at 12:35 a.m. ET on Friday, November 20, 2026, 51.2% below its record of $126,080 from October 6, 2025, CoinGecko data show.
  • It has not broken $60,000. CoinGecko's 24-hour low was $61,273. CoinMarketCap's daily lows last went under $60,000 between June 24 and July 2, 2026.
  • Bitcoin is down about 20.5% since November 1 and below its price on U.S. election day in 2024. U.S. spot bitcoin ETFs have lost $2.79 billion this month.

Why we did this check

Bitcoin kept sliding overnight and traded at $61,568 at 12:35 a.m. ET on Friday, November 20, 2026, down 2.9% in a day and 10.8% in a week, according to CoinGecko. Posts about the drop spread fast, many with round numbers: half the record, below $60,000, back to election day.

We checked six of the most repeated claims against price data from CoinGecko and CoinMarketCap and against U.S. ETF flow data. The table above gives each verdict and the record behind it.

What holds up

The biggest claim is true. Bitcoin is now 51.2% below its October 6, 2025, record of $126,080. It is also below where it stood on November 5, 2024, the day of the U.S. election, and down about 20.5% since November 1.

Ether is further off its own peak: $1,726 against a record of $4,946 from August 24, 2025, down about 65%.

The four-month low is real too. CoinMarketCap's daily data show no low this deep since early July 2026. Its July 3 low was $61,177, just under Thursday's $61,381, and every day from mid-July to November 18 had a higher low.

What does not

Bitcoin has not broken $60,000. The lowest price in CoinGecko's 24-hour window was $61,273, about 2% above that line. CoinMarketCap's daily data put Thursday's low at $61,381. The last time bitcoin traded under $60,000 was late June and early July 2026.

The ETF story is real but overstated. U.S. spot bitcoin funds lost $2.79 billion in November through Thursday, according to CoinMarketCap's flow data. Thursday's first reading, $90.8 million out, was the smallest outflow of the last three days. Wednesday's figure was revised up to $306.2 million out.

LC Insight analysis

This section is our reading of the facts above, not a statement of fact.

  • We think the half-the-record figure will keep spreading because it is true and easy to remember. The $60,000 claim is the one to watch: it is a line, not a fact yet.
  • Many crash posts mix real numbers with ones that are slightly ahead of the data. Checking each against the price feed takes a minute and avoids reacting to a level that has not been hit.

What's next

  • Whether bitcoin trades below $60,000, last seen in early July 2026.
  • Whether the 2026 low of $57,748 from July 1 holds.
  • Friday's ETF flows and any revision to Thursday's $90.8 million outflow.
