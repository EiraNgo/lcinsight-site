Rumor vs record: MARA's $1.1 billion bitcoin sale came before the crash. 5 claims checked

Monday's most shared posts said MARA dumped $1.1 billion of bitcoin into the slide, that bitcoin is deeply oversold and that big holders face record losses. MARA's own filing and CoinGecko's price records show three claims hold up, one is misleading and one cannot be checked.

LC Insight Editorial Team4 min read

Key points

  • Misleading: MARA did sell 13,597 bitcoin for about $1.1 billion, but at an average of about $81,039. CoinGecko shows no daily price that high since October 28, so the sales came before the November slide.
  • True: MARA's filing says $750 million went to fully repay bitcoin-backed loans, freeing 17,477 pledged bitcoin.
  • True: bitcoin's 14-day RSI, worked out from CoinGecko daily prices, was 23.5 to 24.4, and bitcoin sat below its 20-, 50- and 200-day averages.
  • Unverified: the "record $8 billion whale loss" comes from a private model we could not check.

The claims

Bitcoin started Monday, November 16, at about $64,600 on CoinGecko, down 1.3% in 24 hours and 13.1% in seven days. Overnight, posts about the slide spread fast. Many repeated two ideas: that bitcoin miner MARA had dumped $1.1 billion of coins into the crash, and that bitcoin was now deeply "oversold". Others shared a headline about record losses for big holders.

We picked five of the most repeated claims and checked each against MARA's own filing and CoinGecko's price records. We quote the claims, not the people who shared them.

What MARA's filing says

MARA's third-quarter letter to shareholders, filed with the SEC alongside its November 9 earnings call, is the primary record. It says that after the quarter ended on September 30, MARA sold 13,597 bitcoin at an average of about $81,039, for about $1.1 billion. It used $750 million to fully repay its bitcoin-collateralized term loans, and 17,477 pledged bitcoin came back to it.

The letter does not give dates. The price does most of the work. On CoinGecko's daily prices, bitcoin last closed a day at or above $81,039 in the reading stamped 7 p.m. ET on October 28, at $81,274. Since then every daily reading has been lower, and the slide below $70,000 began on November 13. Selling at an average of $81,039 in that later window would have been close to impossible.

At quarter end MARA held 34,012 bitcoin, the letter says, including 19,260 loaned or pledged.

Oversold and below the averages

RSI, the relative strength index, compares the size of recent up days with recent down days on a scale of 0 to 100. We worked it out the standard way, over 14 days, from CoinGecko's daily prices: 24.4 through November 15 and 23.5 with Monday's live price. Traders usually call anything under 30 oversold. It is a measure of how hard the price has fallen, not a forecast.

The moving-average claim also holds. Bitcoin's exponential averages, which give more weight to recent days, stood at $73,941 over 20 days, $77,111 over 50 days and $75,740 over 200 days. CoinGecko's hourly data had bitcoin at $64,536 at 6:42 a.m. ET, below all three. Its low in the past two days was $62,612 at 9 p.m. ET on November 14.

What could not be checked

The "record $8 billion" loss for bitcoin whales comes from a private analytics model that groups wallets by size and estimates their average purchase price. We could not open that data, and a block explorer shows balances, not what a holder paid. So we mark it unverified. The price side is clear: on CoinGecko, bitcoin is 49% below its $126,080 record from October 6, 2025.

LC Insight analysis

This section is our reading of the facts above, not a statement of fact.

  • We think the MARA claim shows how a real number travels without its date. The $1.1 billion sale happened, but the filing's own average price places it before the crash, not inside it.
  • MARA used most of the cash to clear debt that was backed by bitcoin. That removed a lender's claim on 17,477 coins, which cuts the risk of forced selling in a further fall.
  • Oversold readings describe the road behind, not the road ahead. Bitcoin's RSI has been below 30 for days while the price kept slipping.

What's next

  • MARA's next monthly or quarterly bitcoin holdings update
  • Whether bitcoin retests its $62,612 hourly low
  • Whether the 14-day RSI climbs back above 30
