Rumor vs record: Strategy's bitcoin is underwater, but no filing shows it must sell

Bitcoin slipped below Strategy's average purchase price overnight, and posts about a $54 billion bet gone bad spread fast. We checked each line against the company's own SEC filing and CoinGecko prices.

LC Insight Editorial Team4 min read

Key points

  • True: bitcoin traded below Strategy's $75,451 average cost on CoinGecko from about 5 p.m. ET on November 8.
  • Wrong: the pile did not cost $54 billion. Strategy's November 2 filing puts it at $64.08 billion for 849,327 bitcoin.
  • Unverified: "forced to sell." No filing shows a sale, and the latest one shows a purchase and a $4.79 billion cash reserve for dividends and interest.

Why we did this check

Strategy is the largest corporate holder of bitcoin. When bitcoin dipped late on Sunday, November 8, posts and headlines said the company's "$54 billion" bitcoin bet had gone underwater, that it was "$500 million under," and that it might have to sell.

We checked each line against Strategy's latest Form 8-K, filed with the SEC on November 2, and against CoinGecko prices. We do not name the people who posted the claims.

What the filing says

As of November 1, Strategy held 849,327 bitcoin. It paid $64.08 billion for them in total, or $75,451 per coin on average, fees included.

In the week from October 26 to November 1, it bought 811 more bitcoin for $65.5 million, at $80,846 each. The money came from selling 1,178,552 shares of its Class A stock, which raised $164.0 million. The other $98.5 million went to cash. The company also bought back $208.2 million of its STRC preferred stock in that week.

The filing shows a USD Reserve of $4.79 billion, which Strategy says is meant to support dividend payments and interest on its debt, plus $132.7 million of other cash.

What the price did

On CoinGecko's hourly data, bitcoin was near $75,900 for most of Sunday, then fell to about $73,980 by 5 p.m. ET. It touched an hourly low of $73,828 around 3 a.m. ET on Monday and was at $74,554 at 6:41 a.m. ET, down 1.8% in 24 hours.

At that last price, 849,327 bitcoin are worth about $63.32 billion. That is roughly $762 million below what Strategy paid.

Where the claims go wrong

The "$54 billion" figure is about $10 billion short of the cost in the filing. The "$500 million" figure was true only for a short window, because the gap changes by about $849,000 for every $1 move in bitcoin.

The "forced to sell" line has no support in the filing we read. It shows the company buying, not selling, and holding a cash reserve for its payouts. A newer filing could change these totals, and we will update this check if it does.

LC Insight analysis

This section is our reading of the facts above, not a statement of fact.

  • We think "underwater" sounds worse than it is for a company that has not sold. A paper loss matters most if it forces a sale, and the record shows no such step.
  • The size of the gap is the part most posts get wrong. Any figure without a bitcoin price and a time next to it is a snapshot, not a fact.
