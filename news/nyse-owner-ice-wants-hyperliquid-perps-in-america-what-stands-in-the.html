NYSE owner ICE wants Hyperliquid perps in America. What stands in the way?

Intercontinental Exchange plans to run Hyperliquid-based perpetual futures markets for US retail traders with Trade[XYZ], and is taking a minority stake. HYPE jumped almost 20% on the day, but nothing goes live until US regulators approve it.

LC Insight Editorial Team6 min read

Key points

  • ICE, owner of the NYSE, said on October 29, 2026, it will bring Trade[XYZ]'s Hyperliquid-based perpetual futures to US traders, if regulators approve.
  • ICE would run the market and clearinghouse exclusively and is taking an undisclosed minority stake in Trade[XYZ].
  • Kraken's parent Payward is pursuing the same idea through its own regulated exchange. No launch date has been given for either.

The deal

Intercontinental Exchange (ICE), the company that owns the New York Stock Exchange, said on October 29, 2026, that it will work with Trade[XYZ] to offer Trade[XYZ]'s perpetual futures markets to traders inside the United States, under US rules. Trade[XYZ] runs perpetual markets on Hyperliquid that track stocks, stock indices, commodities and currencies. A perpetual future (a "perp") is a leveraged contract with no expiry date, popular with active traders.

According to ICE's press release, ICE will be the exclusive operator of Trade[XYZ]'s regulated markets and will set up a new, dedicated retail market and clearing structure. ICE is also making a strategic minority investment in Trade[XYZ]. It did not disclose the amount and said the stake is not expected to have a material effect on its 2026 adjusted results, capital return or debt reduction plans.

ICE chair and chief executive Jeffrey Sprecher called the perp markets "additive to our futures markets, not a replacement for them." ICE says more than 99% of the participation in its markets today is institutional, so retail trading is a new opportunity for the group.

Who does what

Hyperliquid set out the roles in a post on X. Hyperliquid supplies the technology through HIP-3*, a version of its system that lets outside builders deploy their own perpetual markets. Trade[XYZ] designs and runs the products. ICE operates the regulated market and the clearinghouse. Trade[XYZ]'s existing HIP-3 markets, open to users outside the US, stay as they are.

Once approved, customers who pass identity and anti-money-laundering checks would reach the markets through futures commission merchants (FCMs). These are the regulated brokers that hold client accounts for futures trading, and they would have to join the platform as members, ICE said. ICE also named its joint venture with OKX as a future distribution channel. That depends on the venture's own pending approvals as a US broker-dealer and FCM. ICE made a strategic investment in OKX earlier in 2026.

Both companies will seek approval for the new market and clearing setup, including the approvals needed to offer leveraged trading. Planned products cover crypto, equities, commodities, foreign exchange and other real-world assets.

Trade[XYZ] gave some scale in its own post. It said its markets on Hyperliquid had passed $650 billion in volume in just over a year. It also said billionaire investor Stanley Druckenmiller is joining as an investor, alongside ICE.

Payward got there first, on paper

The ICE news came three days after a related step. On October 26, Hyperliquid said on X that Payward, the parent of the Kraken exchange, intends to be the first CFTC-regulated deployer on HIP-3* markets. Subject to approval, Payward's Bitnomial unit, a CFTC-regulated exchange and clearinghouse, would create and run the markets and clear the contracts. NinjaTrader Clearing, Payward's registered FCM, would carry client accounts.

HIP-3* is the key new piece. Hyperliquid said it adds an optional on-chain allowlist, managed by the deployer, so that only approved accounts can trade. The first release was on testnet, with specifications still subject to change. Hyperliquid described itself as a neutral infrastructure layer and said deployers keep control of, and responsibility for, their own markets.

Payward first laid out the plan in a press release on September 16. It said only accounts onboarded by NinjaTrader and listed on both firms' allowlists could trade. Co-chief executive Arjun Sethi said Payward intends to be first and to carry the regulatory obligations.

How HYPE reacted

Hyperliquid's HYPE token moved sharply. It traded around $79 early on October 29 and reached about $94 by the afternoon, ET, a rise of almost 20%, according to CoinGecko hourly data. That put it within a few percent of its all-time high of $97.96, set on September 23, 2026.

The gain partly faded. HYPE slipped back below $90 on October 31 and stood at about $88.56 on November 2, CoinGecko shows. That was still roughly 9% above its level of about $81 on October 28, the day before the announcement.

Traders are betting on future fee income for Hyperliquid. But none of the regulated markets exist yet, and neither ICE nor Trade[XYZ] has given a launch date. Trade[XYZ] said it would share more on products, eligibility and timing as approvals progress.

LC Insight analysis

This section is our reading of the facts above, not a statement of fact.

  • We think the plumbing matters more than the price. A listed exchange group running clearing for markets built on an on-chain venue is a new model, and it shows other builders how to reach US customers through regulated brokers.
  • Expect a slow rollout. ICE needs approval for both the market and the clearinghouse, and leverage for retail traders is the sensitive part. Think months, not weeks.
  • Payward and ICE are chasing the same idea by different roads. If both get approved, Hyperliquid becomes shared plumbing for several US venues, which will test its claim to be neutral.

What's next

  • Mainnet launch of HIP-3* allowlists and the Payward deployment
  • Approval status of the ICE and OKX joint venture as a broker-dealer and FCM
  • Which US brokers sign up as FCM members
