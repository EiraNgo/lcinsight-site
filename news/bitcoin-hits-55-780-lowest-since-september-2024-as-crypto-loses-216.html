Bitcoin hits $55,780, lowest since September 2024, as crypto loses $216 billion in a day

Bitcoin dropped another 3.2% in one hour late Friday and ether fell to $1,559. Among CoinGecko's 30 largest assets, every asset fell except stablecoins and one tokenized loan product, and the whole market is back under $2 trillion.

LC Insight Editorial Team4 min read

Key points

  • Bitcoin traded at $55,780 at 12:37 a.m. ET on Saturday, November 21, 2026, down 9.4% in 24 hours and 15.7% in seven days, CoinGecko data show. It is 55.8% below its $126,080 record.
  • No daily bitcoin close on CoinMarketCap since September 8, 2024 ($54,842) was lower. Ether, at $1,559, is at its lowest since April 2025.
  • The crypto market fell 9.9% in 24 hours to $1.97 trillion, about $216 billion lost, CoinGecko's global data show. Open bets on Hyperliquid's crypto markets shrank to $2.77 billion from $3.25 billion on Friday morning.

The second leg down

Bitcoin had already broken below $60,000 on Friday morning and closed the U.S. trading day near $58,300, as we reported in our Friday story. Late Friday night it fell again. CoinGecko's hourly prices show bitcoin going from about $58,510 at 10 p.m. ET to about $56,642 at 11 p.m. ET, a 3.2% drop in one hour.

It kept sliding after midnight. At 12:37 a.m. ET on Saturday, November 21, CoinGecko showed bitcoin at $55,780, down 9.4% in 24 hours and 15.7% in seven days. The 24-hour low was $55,615. That is about 4.3% below Friday's 4 p.m. ET level.

The data show when and how fast prices fell, not why. No exchange, fund or issuer we checked has given a reason for the late Friday drop.

Lowest since September 2024

Friday's fall had already taken bitcoin to its lowest price in a year. Saturday's price goes further back. In CoinMarketCap's daily history, the last day bitcoin closed below $55,780 was September 8, 2024, at $54,842. Every daily close since then was higher.

Bitcoin is now 55.8% below its record of $126,080, set on October 6, 2025, according to CoinGecko. In plain terms, someone who bought at the top has lost more than half.

Ether fell from about $1,618 at 10 p.m. ET to about $1,570 at 11 p.m. ET, CoinGecko's hourly chart shows, and traded at $1,559 at 12:37 a.m. ET, down 9.6% in a day and 17.1% in a week. CoinMarketCap's daily history shows no lower ether close since April 10, 2025 ($1,522). Ether is 68.5% below its $4,946 record of August 24, 2025.

Almost nothing held up

CoinGecko's global data put the value of all crypto at $1.97 trillion at 12:30 a.m. ET, down 9.9% in 24 hours. That is roughly $216 billion gone in a day. Trading volume was $129.6 billion. Bitcoin's share of the market was 57.2% and ether's 9.7%.

Among the 30 largest assets on CoinGecko's market list, every cryptocurrency fell. The only ones not down were stablecoins, which track the dollar, and Figure's tokenized home equity loan token, up 0.9%. BNB lost 9.8% to $511.89, XRP 9.5% to $0.872, Solana 9.3% to $65.42 and HYPE 12.7% to $56.80. Tron fell 3.4% and LEO, Bitfinex's exchange token, 1.5%, the smallest drops among large coins.

In the top 100, the deepest falls came in mid-sized coins. Zcash led with a 17.7% drop.

Fewer open bets on Hyperliquid

Hyperliquid is a large exchange for perpetual futures, price bets with no expiry date. Open interest is the value of bets still open. CoinGecko's Hyperliquid tickers, read at 12:41 a.m. ET, put the 178 crypto markets at $2.77 billion of open bets. At 6:31 a.m. ET on Friday they held $3.25 billion, as our Friday story showed, so about $480 million has closed.

Bitcoin's market fell to $1.03 billion from $1.23 billion. HYPE's fell to $527 million from $603 million, and ether's to $468 million from $539 million. Trading in those markets reached $11.3 billion in 24 hours, against $7.85 billion a day earlier. Funding, the small fee that keeps these bets near the spot price, was negative on 53 of the 178 markets, meaning shorts paid longs there.

The tickers do not show liquidations, the forced closing of bets that run out of collateral, so we cannot say how much of the $480 million was forced.

LC Insight analysis

This section is our reading of the facts above, not a statement of fact.

  • We think the late Friday hour matters more than the headline percentage. Bitcoin fell 3.2% in one hour on a Friday night, when U.S. stock markets and ETFs are closed, so the selling happened in crypto's own markets.
  • Open bets on Hyperliquid shrinking by about $480 million while volume rose looks like positions being closed, not new bets piling in. That can leave fewer leveraged traders to force out on a further drop.
  • With bitcoin back at September 2024 prices, almost everyone who bought in the past two years is now underwater. The data do not show a cause, and we would treat any single explanation with care.

What's next

  • Whether bitcoin holds above $54,842, its September 8, 2024 close.
  • Friday's ETF flow figures, and any revisions on Monday.
  • Open interest on Hyperliquid and funding rates over the weekend.
