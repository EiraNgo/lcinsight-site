6 senators ask Treasury to give state stablecoin issuers the same shield as federal applicants

Treasury's draft rule gives federal applicants with a waiver up to 12 months of cover once the stablecoin law takes effect. Three Republicans and three Democrats say state-licensed issuers deserve the same.

LC Insight Editorial Team5 min read

Key points

  • Six senators, three Republicans and three Democrats, wrote to Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent on November 17, 2026, asking for a safe harbor for stablecoin issuers licensed by U.S. states.
  • Treasury's August 18 draft rule lets an issuer with a pending federal application keep going for up to 12 months after the law takes effect, but only if its federal regulator grants a waiver. It offers no matching rule for state issuers.
  • Treasury says the GENIUS Act is expected to take effect January 18, 2027. The senators want the safe harbor to cover issuers in states whose rules are ready by January 18, 2028.

What happened

Six U.S. senators, three Republicans and three Democrats, want stablecoin issuers licensed by states to get the same transition cover Treasury plans for firms waiting on a federal license when the GENIUS Act takes effect, expected January 18, 2027. Senators Cynthia Lummis (R-Wyo.) and Kirsten Gillibrand (D-N.Y.) led a letter dated November 17, 2026 to Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent. Angela Alsobrooks (D-Md.), Kevin Cramer (R-N.D.), Pete Ricketts (R-Neb.) and Mark Warner (D-Va.) also signed. Lummis's office published it on November 18.

The ask is narrow. It covers the moment the GENIUS Act's ban on unlicensed stablecoin issuance starts. The letter asks Treasury to "establish a safe harbor for state issuers."

A payment stablecoin is a token meant to stay worth one dollar and be redeemable for dollars. The GENIUS Act, signed on July 18, 2025, sets the federal rules for who may issue one in the United States. New to the law? Read what the GENIUS Act requires.

What Treasury's draft rule leaves out

Treasury published its proposed rule on stablecoin issuance, offer and sale in the Federal Register on August 18, 2026. It says section 3 of the Act makes it unlawful for anyone other than a permitted issuer to issue a payment stablecoin in the U.S. Treasury writes that the Act's effective date "is expected to be January 18, 2027," 18 months after it was signed.

The draft includes a safe harbor in proposed section 1523.4. It covers a bank subsidiary or a firm with a pending application to become a federal issuer on the effective date, but only if its federal regulator grants a waiver under section 5(f) of the Act. Such a waiver can last up to 12 months from the effective date.

For state-licensed issuers, the draft adopts no transition rule. Instead, its question 74 asks the public whether Treasury should create safe harbors for state issuers before a state certification is filed, while one is pending, or after one is denied. Comments closed October 19, 2026.

What the senators want

  • A state safe harbor. Protect issuers in any state whose stablecoin rules will be ready by January 18, 2028.
  • Parity with federal applicants. Match the protection Treasury plans for pending federal applicants that receive waivers, including from the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency (OCC), the national bank regulator.
  • A real choice of charter. Let issuers choose a state or federal license without fearing their business will be interrupted.

The letter argues that, as drafted, the rule may push issuers toward a federal license, including an OCC license, because it gives them more certainty. The senators call that the opposite of what they intended when they wrote the law.

In the press release, they note that the Act lets an issuer with no more than $10 billion of stablecoins in circulation stay under a state regime, as long as that regime is "substantially similar" to the federal one, and that the Act gave Wyoming, New York and Nebraska an expedited certification process. Treasury set out its principles for judging "substantially similar" state regimes in an April 3, 2026 Federal Register notice.

An earlier letter in June

This is the second letter from a similar group this year. On June 16, 2026, Lummis led seven senators, including Gillibrand, Hagerty, Alsobrooks, Cramer, Cortez Masto and Ricketts, in asking Treasury to protect the states' role in licensing and supervising issuers. That letter asked Treasury to confirm certification stays open to states on an ongoing basis, not as a one-time window, noting that some state legislatures meet only every two years.

The new letter moves from process to timing. A state can only certify once its law is ready, and the federal clock starts January 18. Our earlier story covers the OCC's final rule for federal issuers.

Why it matters for stablecoin users

Most people never see who licenses the company behind their stablecoin. The license matters on the effective date, though. Treasury's draft says issuing without permission becomes unlawful, so an issuer with no license and no safe harbor could not lawfully issue new tokens in the U.S. until it is approved.

Treasury has not said publicly how it will answer question 74, and the final rule has not been published. The senators' letter is a request, not a rule.

LC Insight analysis

This section is our reading of the facts above, not a statement of fact.

  • We think the bipartisan lineup matters: three Republicans and three Democrats who helped pass the law are telling Treasury how they read it. That carries weight in a final rule, but it does not bind Treasury.
  • The likely pressure point is the eight weeks to January 18. A state issuer that cannot count on a safe harbor has a reason to file for a federal license now, which is the shift the senators say they want to avoid.
  • The 2028 date in the letter suggests the senators expect some states to need a full legislative cycle before their rules can be certified.

What's next

  • Treasury's final rule on issuance, offer and sale, and whether it adds a state safe harbor.
  • Whether Wyoming, New York or Nebraska file for certification under the expedited process.
  • Any Treasury reply to the November 17 letter.
