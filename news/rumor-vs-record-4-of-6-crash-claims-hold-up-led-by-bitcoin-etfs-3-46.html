Rumor vs record: 4 of 6 crash claims hold up, led by bitcoin ETFs' $3.46 billion November outflow

A list of crash-week numbers spread widely over the weekend. We checked six lines against CoinMarketCap and CoinGecko: four are true, while the IBIT record and the 60-day Coinbase premium could not be checked.

LC Insight Editorial Team3 min read

Key points

  • U.S. spot bitcoin ETFs lost $3.46 billion from November 2 to 20 in CoinMarketCap's data, matching the viral $3.47 billion. With Monday's $569.7 million in CoinMarketCap's final data, the month is at minus $4.03 billion.
  • Bitcoin's weekend low was $55,149.57 on Saturday, November 21, 56.3% below its $126,080 record. Ether dipped to $1,538.60 the same day.
  • Two claims cannot be checked on our sources: a record $541 million IBIT outflow and 60 straight days of a negative Coinbase premium.

The claims

Over the weekend a list of crash-week numbers spread across crypto social media and trading app feeds: ETF outflows, weekend lows and a fading bounce. We took six of the lines and checked each one against CoinMarketCap and CoinGecko records. The verdicts are in the table above.

We quote the claims as they were shared and do not name the accounts that posted them. A True verdict means the number matches the record. Unverified means we could not test it on primary data, not that it is false.

What holds up

The ETF total checks out. CoinMarketCap's flow series adds up to minus $3.46 billion for the first 15 trading days of November, and minus $3.52 billion once Monday is added. Bitcoin funds had inflows on only two of those 16 days.

The weekend lows also check out. Bitcoin fell to $55,149.57 on Saturday, November 21, in CoinMarketCap's daily data, and ether fell to $1,538.60 in its ether data. CoinGecko lists bitcoin's record at $126,080, so the low sat 56.3% under it.

The bounce did fade. Bitcoin hit $64,478 on Monday and closed at $61,211, almost exactly where it opened. By 12:46 a.m. ET on Tuesday it was at $60,653 on CoinGecko. Spot bitcoin funds lost another $569.7 million on Monday, their fifth outflow day in a row, in CoinMarketCap's final data (corrected from a preliminary $60.4 million).

What we could not check

The $541 million IBIT figure may be right, but CoinMarketCap's series adds all bitcoin funds together, and we could not open per-fund tables. Its worst day this month for all funds combined was $631.9 million on November 19. Calling $541 million a record for one fund needs that fund's full history.

The Coinbase premium line needs a minute-by-minute price comparison across exchanges that none of our sources publish. We mark both as unverified, not wrong.

One line in the list we left out: a stablecoin market total credited to DefiLlama. We could not open DefiLlama's page in time, and we only check claims against pages we have read.

LC Insight analysis

This section is our reading of the facts above, not a statement of fact.

  • We think this list is more accurate than most crash-week posts: every line we could test held up. The two we could not test rest on per-fund and cross-exchange data that most readers cannot see either.
  • The ETF number gets old fast. At $3.52 billion after Monday, quoting $3.47 billion already understates the month.
  • A record claim for one fund should come with that fund's own data. Without it, readers should treat the word "record" as unconfirmed.
