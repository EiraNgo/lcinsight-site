Bitcoin's 4.2% slide to $59,115 came mostly in 3.5 hours Friday morning, then it went flat

CoinGecko's five-minute data shows bitcoin lost about $2,700 between 6:40 and 10:05 a.m. ET Friday, then moved sideways for 20 hours. Ether, XRP, Solana and HYPE fell 3% to 5%. We found no verified single cause.

LC Insight Editorial Team3 min read

Key points

  • Bitcoin traded at $59,115 at 6:36 a.m. ET on November 28, down 4.2% in 24 hours on CoinGecko. Its 24-hour high was $61,713 at 6:40 a.m. ET Friday.
  • Most of the drop came early: bitcoin hit $59,033 by 10:05 a.m. ET Friday, then traded between about $59,500 and $60,200 until the early hours of Saturday.
  • Ether fell 4.9% to $1,643, XRP 4.0% to $0.94, Solana 3.5% to $70.74 and HYPE 5.4% to $62.40. U.S. spot bitcoin ETFs lost $389.0 million on Friday.

When the drop happened

A 4.2% daily loss sounds like a rough night. The five-minute data on CoinGecko tells a calmer story. Bitcoin stood at $61,713 at 6:40 a.m. ET on Friday, November 27. By 10:05 a.m. ET it was at $59,033, a fall of about $2,700, or 4.3%, in three and a half hours.

After that it barely moved. Through Friday afternoon and evening it traded between about $59,500 and $60,200. At 10:40 p.m. ET it was at $59,912. By 6:35 a.m. ET Saturday it had slipped to $59,109, about 1.3% lower than late Friday and close to Friday morning's low, but not below it.

The rest of the market

Larger coins moved in step, per CoinGecko market data at 6:36 a.m. ET. Ether was at $1,643.47, down 4.9%, after a five-minute low of $1,641.55 at 10:10 a.m. ET Friday. XRP was at $0.9375 (down 4.0%), Solana at $70.74 (down 3.5%) and Hyperliquid's HYPE at $62.40 (down 5.4%).

On a seven-day view most of these coins are still up: bitcoin by 2.2%, XRP by 4.8%, Solana by 4.0% and HYPE by 7.1%. The big outlier of the night was World Liberty Financial's WLFI, down 53%, which is a separate story.

Trading rose, but did not surge

Bitcoin's rolling 24-hour volume on CoinGecko rose from about $23.3 billion on Friday morning to a peak near $30.2 billion at 3:15 p.m. ET, then eased back to $25.2 billion by Saturday morning. Ether's followed the same shape, from $9.1 billion to about $11.8 billion at 5:35 p.m. ET and back to $10.1 billion. That is a rise of about 30%, not a burst of panic trading.

No verified cause yet

We found no verified single cause, such as an official announcement, a hack of a major platform or a policy decision, behind Friday morning's drop. One measurable pressure ran the same way: U.S. spot bitcoin ETFs lost $389.0 million on Friday in CoinMarketCap's final data, their eighth straight outflow day. That is a fact about the funds, not proof that they moved the price.

LC Insight analysis

This section is our reading of the facts above, not a statement of fact.

  • We think the 24-hour figure overstates the stress. Most of the fall came in one burst on Friday morning, and the price then moved sideways for about 20 hours.
  • Bitcoin is sitting just above Friday's $59,033 low, the level we are watching next.
  • Steady ETF outflows are the clearest flow we can measure right now. They have run the same way as the price for eight sessions, but the data does not show which drove which.

What's next

  • Whether bitcoin holds above Friday's $59,033 low.
  • Monday's ETF flows, the first full session after Thanksgiving.
  • Whether WLFI's slide spreads to other tokens.
